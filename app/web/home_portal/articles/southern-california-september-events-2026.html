November 2026 event guide

Thirty Southern California fall plans worth building a day around.

Today, November 21, compare the Route 66 Great Los Angeles Walk, The Huntington Holiday Market, Long Beach's Dreamstate festival and Aquarium Autumn Festival, Burbank's Winter Wine Walk, The Broad's free Has the myth been shattered exhibition, and CTN Animation Expo. Sunday adds Pasadena's free Doo Dah Love-In, Santa Monica's free tree lighting, Descanso Gardens' Enchanted Forest of Light, and Beverly Hills' free Holiday Craft Fair. The LA Auto Show, L.A. Zoo Lights, Enchant Christmas, Swiss Winter Village, the Old Pasadena Holiday Passport, Little Women, the newly open Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center, the Pershing Square rink, NightTales, and other long-run exhibitions provide flexible alternatives. Recommendations are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Holiday Ice Rink at Pershing Square: best flexible holiday skating plan

When: November 12-January 3, 2027; weekdays 3-10 p.m., weekends noon-10 p.m., with expanded holiday-week hours. Best for: families, date nights, first-time skaters, downtown visitors, transit riders, and groups that want a holiday activity with many possible dates.

Downtown Los Angeles' 7,200-square-foot outdoor rink opened November 12 for 60-minute sessions that begin every 90 minutes. General admission includes skate rental; lockers, skate helpers, and gloves cost extra. This leads the guide because it is newly open, runs daily, and is easier to schedule than a one-night program. Reserve a session before building dinner around it, arrive early enough to fit skates, and use the Pershing Square Metro station when practical. The organizer lists different noon opening hours during Thanksgiving week and the late-December school break, so check the selected date rather than relying on a general schedule.

2. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

3. Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center: best new science landmark

When: Open daily after its November 13 debut; reserve a timed arrival before visiting. Best for: families, spaceflight and engineering fans, school groups, museum travelers, and visitors who want to see Space Shuttle Endeavour in its permanent launch configuration.

The California Science Center's 200,000-square-foot expansion nearly doubles its exhibition space with 100 aerospace artifacts and 100 hands-on exhibits. Its centerpiece is the world's only complete, authentic space-shuttle system displayed vertically in launch position: Endeavour, solid rocket boosters, and the last flight-qualified external tank. This ranks near the top because it is a major permanent opening rather than a one-day program. General museum admission is free, but the Science Center strongly recommends timed reservations for the new galleries because entry without one may be limited; a $4 reservation fee applies to nonmembers.

Reserve through the California Science Center's official ticket page, review the Science Center Foundation's official opening announcement and exhibit details, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

When: November 15-29; pick up a passport from a participating Old Pasadena retailer. Best for: small-business shoppers, neighborhood explorers, groups that enjoy a low-cost challenge, holiday gift browsers, and locals who want a reason to explore beyond familiar blocks.

It ranks highly because the November 29 deadline leaves nearly two weeks to finish and the route can become a self-paced neighborhood shopping day. Ask a participating store for the current passport and redemption instructions, check each retailer's hours before making a route, and treat any shopping as optional rather than the price of participation.

When: November 20-21, 4 p.m.-1 a.m.; official afterparties run midnight-6 a.m. Best for: adult electronic-music audiences, dedicated trance fans, visual-production enthusiasts, and groups prepared for a long waterfront festival night.

When: November 19-22; access and programming vary by day and pass type. Best for: animation students, working artists, independent creators, studio professionals, portfolio builders, and visual storytellers seeking focused career connections.

CTN brings artists and industry professionals to the Burbank Airport Marriott for screenings, panels, workshops, portfolio reviews, networking, exhibitions, and daily creative programming. 8. Great Los Angeles Walk: best free Route 66 endurance day

When: November 21; the full walk starts in Echo Park at 9 a.m. and ends at the Santa Monica Pier. Best for: experienced urban walkers, Route 66 history fans, neighborhood explorers, endurance groups, and participants comfortable spending most of a day on foot.

The 21st annual walk follows the full length of Santa Monica Boulevard for the Route 66 centennial, crossing Los Angeles County before a finish celebration and photo opportunity at the Pier's End of the Trail sign. It is a distinctive free way to experience many neighborhoods, but it is not a casual short stroll. Register with the organizer, study the current route and exit points, wear broken-in shoes, carry water and sun protection, and be prepared to leave the route if distance, weather, darkness, or traffic conditions make continuing unsafe. Arrange an E Line or other transit trip home rather than expecting to return to the start on foot.

When: Select nights November 12-January 10, 2027. Best for: museum regulars, date nights, families with older children, light-art audiences, natural-history fans, and visitors who want an evening alternative to a daytime gallery visit.

It earns a high rank because it combines art, science, and technology in a new evening format rather than simply extending normal museum hours. Visitors sensitive to bright or changing light and amplified sound should review access details with the museum before booking.

When: Opens September 22; timed admission is required. Best for: movie lovers, families, visual-storytelling fans, architecture visitors, and anyone choosing one major new Los Angeles museum this fall.

When: November 21-April 4, 2027. Best for: contemporary-art audiences, media and history students, first-time Broad visitors, social-justice readers, and groups seeking a free long-run downtown museum plan.

21. Where History Meets the Road in Pasadena: best Route 66 centennial exhibition

When: June 13-January 24, 2027; Thursday-Sunday, noon-5 p.m. Best for: Route 66 travelers, Pasadena-history readers, design and transportation audiences, families with older children, and visitors who prefer a flexible museum date.

Pasadena Museum of History traces how Route 66 and Colorado Boulevard shaped mobility, commerce, roadside culture, architecture, and preservation through photographs, memorabilia, oral histories, and a 70-foot illustrated scroll following the highway from Santa Monica to Needles. General admission is $9, seniors pay $7, and museum members, students, and visitors under 18 enter free. The long run makes this a lower-pressure alternative to a one-day centennial program. Allow about 60-90 minutes, check the museum's current hours before leaving, and pair the exhibition with one Colorado Boulevard stop rather than turning the centennial into a cross-city checklist.

Admission is free, but gallery access can change around special events.
