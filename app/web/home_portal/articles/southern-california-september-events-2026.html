October 2026 event guide

Forty Southern California fall plans worth building a day around.

Start with Halloween at Kidspace, Spider Pavilion, Huntington Beach Oktoberfest, Queen Mary's Dark Harbor, Carved at Descanso Gardens, the final Long Beach Open Studio Tour day, Pasadena's Bark in the Park, Marina Spooktacular, Burbank's Wild Creatures Walk, Grand Park's Downtown Día de los Muertos, LA Plaza's Día de los Muertos Family Day, UCLA's Party on the Pier, Fright Dive, Long Beach's Día de los Muertos festivals, Anaheim's Día de los Muertos night, Boo at the L.A. Zoo, West Hollywood Halloween Carnaval, Battleship IOWA's Halloween STEMebration, Día de los Muertos at the Arboretum in Arcadia, Beverly Hills' Fall Funtastic, West Hollywood's Floating Pumpkin Patch, Olvera Street's nine-night celebration, Coast Film & Music Festival, The Huntington's Strange Science, Santa Monica Pier's Día de los Muertos celebration, Forest Lawn Glendale's Día de los Muertos event, Self Help Graphics' block party, Halloween Horror Nights, and Fleurs de Villes at Greystone. Flexible museum, theater, and exhibition picks extend the guide beyond Halloween. These recommendations are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Halloween at Kidspace in Pasadena: best repeatable young-family fall visit

When: September 18-October 31. Best for: families with younger children, costume lovers, museum members, and groups that want seasonal play without a late-night haunted attraction.

Kidspace layers costume parades at 11 a.m., 1 p.m., and 3 p.m. onto a spooky trike house, Adopt-a-Monster workshop, Witch's Kitchen, and pumpkin bowling. The program is included with museum admission, so it delivers better value as a full Kidspace day than as a quick Halloween photo stop; members receive the seasonal programming with their regular access. Reserve admission for the date you want, check adjusted hours before leaving, and choose Dark Harbor instead if your group wants after-dark scares rather than child-centered play.

Check Kidspace's official program, daily parade times, and tickets, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

2. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

3. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

4. Queen Mary's Dark Harbor: best long-running Halloween night

When: Select nights September 18-November 1. Best for: horror fans, thrill-seeking groups, Halloween-season visitors, and people who want mazes, live entertainment, rides, and themed food in one waterfront event.

The Queen Mary and surrounding harbor become a large after-dark festival with haunted mazes, hundreds of performers, immersive attractions, carnival rides, themed bars and lounges, food, and optional VIP experiences. The variety makes this a stronger all-evening choice than a single haunted house, but tickets are sold online and the ship's calendar controls which nights are active. Buy only after checking the selected night's entry time, ticket tier, age guidance, attraction availability, and current parking instructions.

Review the Queen Mary's official fall program, check the current parking and shuttle notice, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

5. Carved at Descanso Gardens: best repeatable illuminated fall walk

When: October 2-November 1, 6-10 p.m. Best for: families, pumpkin-carving fans, garden visitors, photographers, date nights, and groups that need several evening dates to compare.

Descanso Gardens builds a one-mile evening route around glowing jack-o'-lanterns, giant hand-carved pumpkins, illuminated railroad displays, whimsical wood spirits, and seasonal installations. The monthlong run makes Carved more flexible than a one-night festival, while the garden setting gives it a different rhythm from a haunted maze. Member adult tickets range from $22-$45 and non-member adult tickets from $29-$52, with lower child pricing and free admission for children younger than 2. Popular times can sell out, so choose a timed entry before planning dinner; parking is free first come, with limited $10 valet parking.

Review Descanso Gardens' official experience, dates, parking, and Halloween-night notes, check the official hours, ticket ranges, and sellout guidance, then browse 100 nearby Glendale listings or compare nearby restaurants.

6. Long Beach Open Studio Tour: best free artist-led neighborhood crawl

When: Final Eastside weekend October 24-25, 1-5 p.m. Best for: art buyers, studio-process fans, cyclists, neighborhood explorers, and visitors who prefer a self-directed afternoon to a single gallery.

More than 120 artists across more than 80 studios participate across four free weekends, with the final Eastside route now remaining. The neighborhood focus is the advantage: visitors can build a practical cluster instead of trying to cover the entire city. Use the organizer's interactive map, limit the first pass to three or four studios, and check individual notes for accessibility, alley entrances, live music, or workshops. Walking or biking works for tighter clusters; a car is more practical when studios are spread out.

Use the official tour overview and interactive neighborhood maps, confirm the four-weekend schedule and 1-5 p.m. hours, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

7. Bark in the Park in Pasadena: best free dog-friendly Halloween meetup

When: October 25, 11 a.m.-2 p.m. Best for: dog owners, families, costume-contest fans, neighborhood visitors, and groups seeking a free daytime Halloween plan.

Playhouse Village Park hosts a Howl-o-ween parade and costume contest with treat stations, pup bites, and activities for all ages. The compact three-hour format makes this an easy add-on to a Pasadena lunch or museum visit, while the pet focus distinguishes it from the city's other family Halloween programs. Arrive with a leash, water, waste bags, and a plan for how your dog handles costumes and crowds; visitors without dogs can still enjoy the parade, but owners should skip the event if noise or close contact is stressful for their animal.

Confirm the free event's date, hours, location, and current program with Visit Pasadena, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

8. Marina Spooktacular: best free waterfront Halloween night for mixed ages

When: October 25, 4-10 p.m. Best for: families arriving before dark, older children and adults seeking higher-scare zones, waterfront visitors, and groups that want a free evening program.

Burton Chace Park combines face painting, crafts, a digital photo booth, a graveyard treasure hunt, carnival games, a haunted blackout maze, and scare zones. The range is the advantage: younger families can focus on the early activities while thrill-seekers stay for the darker elements. Admission is free with RSVP, but every attendee must complete a liability waiver. Nearby public lots usually cost $7-$10, accessible paths and restrooms are available, and the $1 Marina WaterBus runs until 11 p.m. Arrive early because parking and popular activities can fill.

Review the Marina del Rey Tourism Board's official hours, RSVP and waiver rules, activity mix, parking, transit, and accessibility guidance, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare nearby restaurants.

9. Burbank Wild Creatures Walk: best short docent-led nature outing

When: October 25, 11:30 a.m.-12:30 p.m. Best for: families, local hikers, wildlife learners, and Burbank groups looking for a compact daytime plan before lunch.

A docent leads an all-ages walk from Stough Canyon Nature Center through the Verdugo Mountains, using the local landscape to explain the animals that live there. It ranks below the larger festivals because the program is only one hour, but that short format and the city's strong directory coverage make it practical for residents who want a low-commitment outdoor option. Register through the city-linked recreation system, wear trail shoes, carry water and sun protection, and check weather or trail notices before leaving.

Confirm the City of Burbank's official date, hours, location, age guidance, and registration link, then browse 968 Burbank listings or compare Burbank restaurants.

10. Grand Park's Downtown Día de los Muertos: best free long-run remembrance

When: October 24-November 2, 8 a.m.-9 p.m.; opening celebration October 24, 11 a.m.-4 p.m.; closing celebration November 1, 3-7 p.m. Best for: families, cultural learners, altar and public-art visitors, music audiences, and groups that need a free event with flexible dates.

Gloria Molina Grand Park becomes a 10-day outdoor exhibition of community ofrendas, with an opening celebration tied to Grand Ave Arts: All Access and a closing Noche de los Muertos program. The organizer describes live music, an Indigenous-rooted processional and blessing, face painting, sugar-skull workshops, wellness activities, and opportunities to contribute to a community altar. It ranks above a one-night festival for flexibility and cultural focus, but the opening and closing programs will be busier than weekday altar viewing. Use Metro when practical, bring only permitted items, and approach the installations as places of remembrance rather than seasonal props.

Review Grand Park's official dates, program, transit, accessibility, bag, and parking guidance, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

11. LA Plaza Día de los Muertos Family Day: best free hands-on culture program

When: October 25, noon-4 p.m.; community ofrendas remain on view through November 3. Best for: families, cultural learners, live-performance audiences, craft participants, and downtown visitors who want a museum-led free event.

LA Plaza de Cultura y Artes pairs live Oaxacan music and dance with mask painting, flower-accessory making, memory-focused herb bundles, a community ofrenda, and food for purchase. It earns a high rank because the free workshops explain traditions instead of using Día de los Muertos as decoration. Advance RSVP is recommended, but entry is still subject to capacity and walk-ins are accommodated only when space permits. Arrive near noon if children want to complete the workshop series, and budget separately for food.

12. UCLA Party on the Pier in Santa Monica: best family fundraiser with rides and play

When: October 25, 10 a.m.-2 p.m. Best for: families, Pacific Park ride fans, supporters of children's health, and groups comfortable with premium fundraiser pricing.

UCLA Mattel Children's Hospital's 26th annual fundraiser combines Pacific Park rides, carnival games, celebrity photo opportunities, meal vouchers, treats, crafts, a play zone, and UCLA People-Animal Connection therapy dogs. The concentration of family activities and hospital mission are compelling, but this is a premium benefit rather than a budget pier day, and some games or experiences require separate tickets. Purchase admission before traveling, budget for add-ons, bring a card because the event is cashless, and use transit when possible to avoid pier traffic.

13. Burbank Halloween Carnival: best low-cost neighborhood game night for ages 3-11

When: October 31, 6:30-8 p.m. Best for: families with children ages 3-11, carnival-game fans, younger trick-or-treaters, and Burbank residents seeking a short city-run Halloween plan.

McCambridge Recreation Center hosts a 90-minute carnival built around games of luck and skill. The city lists admission at $5 per child and targets ages 3-11, making this a more focused and lower-cost choice than a theme-park haunt. It ranks above broader Halloween attractions for affordability and a clearly defined audience, but families should not expect an all-evening festival. Confirm capacity and current entry instructions before leaving, arrive near the start for the widest game selection, and use the city's free Skatefest on October 30 if an all-ages activity is a better fit.

When: October 30, 7:30-11 p.m. Best for: adults 18 and older, live-music and DJ audiences, aquarium fans, Halloween date nights, and groups seeking an indoor late-night event.

The Aquarium's Night Dive series becomes Fright Dive for Halloween, combining after-hours gallery access with live music, DJs, art, culture, food, and drinks. It ranks below the all-ages Scarium because the official page had not yet posted the full Fright Dive lineup at this review, but the established 18-and-older format and aquarium setting make it a useful October 30 option. General tickets are $49.95 and member tickets are $44.95; food and drinks cost extra, and alcohol is limited to guests 21 and older with identification. Purchase early because the Aquarium warns that Night Dive events often sell out.

The City of Long Beach and the Office of First District Councilwoman Mary Zendejas return the celebration to Rainbow Lagoon in its original festival format. The city promises culturally rooted performances, vendors, a resource fair, and participation from city departments and community partners. It ranks as a strong no-admission follow-on to MOLAA's October 25 festival because it offers six hours of programming in a central waterfront location, but the city says more details will be released closer to the date. Confirm the final schedule, access, parking, and transit before leaving, and approach the gathering as a remembrance of loved ones rather than a generic seasonal festival.

The City of Anaheim's annual Día de los Muertos: A Night of Community & Culture brings altars, live performances, food, and family activities to the downtown civic campus. It ranks above a generic Halloween stop because remembrance and community culture are the center of the program, while free admission leaves room in the budget for food. 17. Boo at the L.A. Zoo: best admission-included family trick-or-treat day

When: October 24-25 and October 31-November 1, 10 a.m.-5 p.m.; trick-or-treating 10 a.m.-3 p.m. Best for: families, animal lovers, costume groups, conservation learners, zoo members, and visitors who want Halloween activities inside a full daytime attraction.

The City of Los Angeles-operated Zoo adds six trick-or-treat stations, keeper talks, pumpkin animal enrichment, themed photo stops, live performances, games, an inflatable corn maze, and a conservation expo to four regular zoo days. The event programming is included with paid admission or an active membership, though pumpkin painting costs extra and candy is available while supplies last. Bring a trick-or-treat bag, review the no-full-face-mask and no-weapon costume policy, and treat the Halloween layer as part of a normal zoo visit rather than a stand-alone evening event.

18. West Hollywood Halloween Carnaval: best free large-scale costume night

When: October 31, 6-11 p.m. Best for: adult costume fans, LGBTQ+ nightlife audiences, live-DJ crowds, photographers, and visitors comfortable with dense pedestrian streets.

The city closes a mile of Santa Monica Boulevard between Doheny and La Cienega for costumes, a live-DJ stage at San Vicente, food trucks, and business celebrations across the Rainbow District. Free admission and the scale of the crowd make it the region's strongest street-party choice, but it is not a gentle family fallback: the city discourages children and pets, bans alcohol and public drinking, allows clear plastic bags rather than backpacks or totes, and closes major streets and nearby parking facilities. Use transit or the free event shuttle, travel light, arrange a group meeting point, and check the city page again for final transportation updates.

19. Walt Disney Archives and Disneyland art at Muzeo: best flexible Anaheim museum stop

When: Wednesday-Sunday, 11 a.m.-5 p.m., through November 1. Best for: animation fans, artists, families, Disney-history readers, and Anaheim visitors who want a lower-cost indoor culture plan away from the theme parks.

One admission covers three complementary exhibitions: the Walt Disney Archives' world-premiere Capturing Life, Creating Character uses rarely seen photographs, materials, and interactives to explain how live performers informed animated movement; Anaheimland tells Disneyland history through local workers, neighbors, memorabilia, and oral histories; and The Happiest Place: SHAG Imagines Disneyland focuses on the artist's mid-century interpretations of familiar attractions. The combination is the reason to go—start with the animation-process exhibition, then choose local history or design according to your group. General admission is $10 for ages 14 and older, $8 for Anaheim residents, $7 for seniors and children ages 4-13, and $1 for up to four visitors presenting a qualifying EBT card and photo ID. The museum validates parking in Car Parks 3 and 5 and does not permit large bags or strollers in the galleries.

Read Muzeo's official Walt Disney Archives exhibition overview, compare all three current exhibitions, check admission, accessibility, bags, and validated parking, then browse 35 Anaheim listings or compare Anaheim restaurants.

20. Lucas Museum opening in Los Angeles: best new cultural landmark

When: Opens September 22; timed admission is required. Best for: movie lovers, families, visual-storytelling fans, architecture visitors, and anyone choosing one major new Los Angeles museum this fall.

The museum's official opening announcement confirms more than 1,300 works across over 30 galleries, a continuous film program, a free research library, Skywalker Grill, and a new 13-acre park in Exposition Park. Adult admission is $25, visitors 17 and younger enter free, and several other free and reduced-price categories are published on the ticket page. This ranks as the month's strongest new indoor culture pick, but the 100,000 square feet of galleries makes a focused first visit smarter than trying to absorb everything: choose two or three collection themes, reserve a timed slot, and leave time for the building and gardens.

Read the museum's official opening announcement, check current ticket prices and eligibility, then use 61 Los Angeles directory listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

21. Where History Meets the Road in Pasadena: best Route 66 centennial exhibition

When: September 26-January 24, 2027; Thursday-Sunday, noon-5 p.m. Best for: Route 66 travelers, Pasadena-history readers, design and transportation audiences, families with older children, and visitors who prefer a flexible museum date.

Pasadena Museum of History traces how Route 66 and Colorado Boulevard shaped mobility, commerce, roadside culture, architecture, and preservation through photographs, memorabilia, and interpretive displays. General admission is $9, seniors pay $7, and museum members, students, and visitors under 18 enter free. The four-month run makes this a lower-pressure alternative to a timed Walktober route. Allow about 60-90 minutes, check the museum's current hours before leaving, and pair the exhibition with one Colorado Boulevard stop rather than turning the centennial into a cross-city checklist.

Review Visit Pasadena's official dates, admission, hours, and exhibition overview, explore the Route 66 centennial guide, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

22. Portraits: Faces and Waves in Santa Monica: best free flexible photography stop

When: October 3-March 27, 2027; daily except special events. Best for: photography audiences, Pier visitors, coastal-history readers, portraiture students, and groups needing a quieter free alternative to a timed festival.

The Annenberg Community Beach House Gallery pairs Sam Comen's portraits of 274 Santa Monica Pier visitors with Anthony Friedkin's photographs of waves made near the Beach House and State Beach. The local focus gives the exhibition more context than a generic gallery stop: one body of work records who came to the Pier during America's 250th year, while the other treats individual waves as short-lived forms. Admission is free, but gallery access can change around special events. Check same-day hours, allow 30-60 minutes, and combine the show with the beach or one nearby meal rather than expecting a full museum campus.

Read the City of Santa Monica's official dates, artist context, location, and access note, continue to the ranked Santa Monica October guide, then browse Santa Monica listings or compare Santa Monica restaurants.

The second rotation of The Huntington's Laura Aguilar presentation focuses on a lesser-known early-1990s series documenting Día de los Muertos celebrations in Los Angeles homes and public gatherings, including artists, couples, and families active in the city's Chicano art community. The long run makes this a better choice than a one-weekend event for visitors who want time with both the galleries and gardens. Reserve general admission for a non-Tuesday visit, start with the photography installation while attention is fresh, and leave enough time for the wider campus rather than treating it as a quick standalone stop.

The companion Zombies! gallery is designed for all ages, but the main exhibition includes disturbing themes and the museum recommends parental guidance. 26. Fleurs de Villes at Greystone: best limited-run floral-and-fashion exhibition

When: October 28-November 4; timed tickets required. Best for: floral-design and costume fans, The Gilded Age viewers, photographers, historic-home visitors, and groups seeking an indoor Beverly Hills plan.

32. Día de los Muertos on Olvera Street: best free nine-night tradition

When: October 25-November 2, 10 a.m.-9 p.m.; La Danza de la Muerte at 6 p.m. and the nightly Novenario procession at 7 p.m. Best for: families, cultural-history learners, altar visitors, evening procession audiences, and groups that need several free dates.

Olvera Street's merchants and El Pueblo Historical Monument organize nine days of community altars, a theatrical account of the tradition's Indigenous roots, Mayan blessings, Aztec dancers, living-muertos participants, and a nightly ceremony honoring a community member or merchant family.
