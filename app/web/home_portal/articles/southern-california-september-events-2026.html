October 2026 event guide

Thirty-two Southern California fall plans worth building a day around.

Start with Pacific Airshow, Hot Wheels Monster Trucks at Long Beach Arena, Little Women Ballet at Heritage Square Museum, the free Los Angeles Korean Festival, Tustin Tiller Days, Burbank Book Festival, Long Beach Open Studio Tour, Latino Restaurant Week, Santa Monica's Route 66 Art Walk and Continuum celebration, West Hollywood's free park movie, Glendale Cultural Festival, ArtNight Pasadena, Pasadena's Latino Heritage Parade & Festival and Fall Festival, Indigenous Pride LA, Burbank's Haunted Adventure, Long Beach Marathon weekend, and the Beverly Hills Art Show. Flexible museum, theater, Halloween, concert, and exhibition picks extend the guide through October and beyond. These recommendations are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Pacific Airshow in Huntington Beach: best immediate spectacle

When: October 2-4; gates at 9 a.m., flying approximately 10:30 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: aviation fans, families comparing beach and ticketed viewing, photographers, STEM-minded children, and groups that can handle a long, loud outdoor day.

The tenth-anniversary show brings the U.S. Air Force Thunderbirds, Canadian Forces Snowbirds, F-35 demonstrations, heritage aircraft, civilian aerobatics, performer signings, a kids zone, and hands-on STEM programming to the Huntington Beach shoreline. It ranks above the weekend's smaller festivals because four or more hours of flying can fill a day, but value depends on the view you choose: the official precinct includes commentary and ground programming, while ticket prices, zones, and sightlines vary. Bring hearing protection for sensitive guests, sun and sand gear, and a backup plan for weather or schedule changes; the organizer releases the final daily sequence each morning.

Review the organizer's official dates, estimated flying hours, access, and family guidance, compare current ticket zones and prices, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

2. Hot Wheels Monster Trucks in Long Beach: best indoor family spectacle

When: October 3 at 11:30 a.m. and 6:30 p.m.; October 4 at 1:30 p.m. Best for: families with vehicle-loving children, stunt-show fans, groups needing an indoor weather backup, and visitors who prefer a defined two-hour-style arena outing to an open-ended festival.

The Glow-N-Fire tour brings Mega Wrex, Tiger Shark, Bone Shaker, Bigfoot, Skelesaurus, Rhinomite, FMX riders, and a fire-breathing car-crushing robot dinosaur to Long Beach Arena. Three showtimes make it easier to fit around a meal than the waterfront's all-day options, but the effects and engine noise can be intense. Children age 2 and older need tickets; compare the base seat price before considering the optional floor-level pre-show or VIP add-ons, bring hearing protection for sensitive guests, and leave extra time for arena parking and security.

Confirm Long Beach Arena's official showtimes, ticket rules, and parking link, compare the tour organizer's current event listing, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

3. Little Women Ballet in Los Angeles: best immersive literary performance

When: October 2-4, with five performances at Heritage Square Museum. Best for: ballet newcomers, Louisa May Alcott readers, families with older children, historic-home fans, and audiences who want a participatory performance rather than a conventional stage view.

Professional dancers move the March sisters' story through Heritage Square's Victorian houses in close-up vignettes, with guests invited into parts of the action and Victorian social dancing. The setting makes this more intimate and distinctive than a standard seated ballet, but it also means moving through historic buildings rather than staying in one auditorium. Choose the date and time before planning dinner, wear shoes suited to walking between spaces, and review ticket availability and accessibility directly with the presenter.

Confirm Heritage Square Museum's October 2-4 program, read the company's explanation of the immersive format, compare Discover Los Angeles' current performance listing, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

4. Halloween at Kidspace in Pasadena: best repeatable young-family fall visit

When: September 18-October 31. Best for: families with younger children, costume lovers, museum members, and groups that want seasonal play without a late-night haunted attraction.

Kidspace layers costume parades at 11 a.m., 1 p.m., and 3 p.m. onto a spooky trike house, Adopt-a-Monster workshop, Witch's Kitchen, and pumpkin bowling. The program is included with museum admission, so it delivers better value as a full Kidspace day than as a quick Halloween photo stop; members receive the seasonal programming with their regular access. Reserve admission for the date you want, check adjusted hours before leaving, and choose Dark Harbor instead if your group wants after-dark scares rather than child-centered play.

Check Kidspace's official program, daily parade times, and tickets, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

5. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

6. Into the Woods in Costa Mesa: best full-scale musical-theater choice

When: Through October 18. Best for: Sondheim fans, theater groups, older children, date nights, and visitors who want a substantial indoor performance with several dates to compare.

South Coast Repertory's fortieth-anniversary production runs about two hours and 35 minutes with an intermission and is recommended for ages 10 and older; fog, haze, and strobe effects are used. This is the guide's strongest long-run musical choice because the remaining schedule offers both matinees and evenings. Pick a matinee for a family or dining-centered day, check the exact seat price before committing, and allow enough time for the full performance rather than pairing it with another major event.

Compare South Coast Repertory's official dates, tickets, age guidance, and access performance, read the theater's production and parking notes, then browse 34 Costa Mesa listings or compare Costa Mesa restaurants.

7. The Visit at Pasadena Playhouse: best dark-comedy theater choice

When: Through October 4. Best for: theater audiences, dark-comedy fans, date nights, and parents or disabled patrons choosing a specifically supported performance.

Friedrich Dürrenmatt's comedy follows a wealthy woman who offers to rescue her struggling hometown on an impossible condition. Pasadena Playhouse's production runs one hour and 45 minutes without intermission and includes theatrical haze, flashing lights, and prop guns. The remaining performances continue through October 4; compare the exact date, access services, childcare options, and ticket inventory before building dinner around the show.

Use Pasadena Playhouse's official schedule, tickets, runtime, childcare, access, and content guidance, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

8. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

9. Queen Mary's Dark Harbor: best long-running Halloween night

When: Select nights September 18-November 1. Best for: horror fans, thrill-seeking groups, Halloween-season visitors, and people who want mazes, live entertainment, rides, and themed food in one waterfront event.

The Queen Mary and surrounding harbor become a large after-dark festival with haunted mazes, hundreds of performers, immersive attractions, carnival rides, themed bars and lounges, food, and optional VIP experiences. The variety makes this a stronger all-evening choice than a single haunted house, but tickets are sold online and the ship's calendar controls which nights are active. Buy only after checking the selected night's entry time, ticket tier, age guidance, attraction availability, and current parking instructions.

Review the Queen Mary's official fall program, check the current parking and shuttle notice, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

10. Los Angeles Korean Festival: best free four-day culture weekend

When: October 1-4; festival grounds open daily at 10 a.m. Best for: Korean-food fans, live-performance audiences, families, Koreatown visitors, and groups that want a free festival with several days to choose from.

The 53rd annual festival fills Seoul International Park with traditional and contemporary performance, Korean food, cultural exhibits, shopping, family activities, and community booths. Free admission and four full days make this the guide's strongest broad-access October festival, but food, shopping, and parking are separate costs. Choose Thursday or Friday for a lighter first look, or the weekend for the fullest atmosphere; use Metro or the organizer's continuous parking shuttles because the published lots are cashless and neighborhood traffic will be heavy.

Review Discover Los Angeles' current free-admission overview, confirm the organizer's October 1-4 dates and operating hours, check cashless parking and shuttle guidance, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

11. Tustin Tiller Days: best all-weekend Orange County community festival

When: October 2, 4-11 p.m.; October 3, 11 a.m.-11 p.m.; October 4, noon-8 p.m. Best for: families, parade fans, carnival groups, live-entertainment audiences, and visitors who want several distinct ways to join one community weekend.

The City of Tustin's 68th annual celebration combines a Friday-through-Sunday carnival with two entertainment stages, contests, food, vendors, a Saturday pancake breakfast, and a 10 a.m. Old Town parade. Saturday carries the fullest program, while Friday evening or Sunday afternoon is easier for a shorter visit. Carnival rides, the $10 breakfast, food, and vendor purchases require separate budgets; the city lists $40 advance unlimited-ride wristbands, so compare that price with how long your group actually plans to ride.

Review the City of Tustin's official dates and hours, check the city's parade, breakfast, and ride-wristband details, then browse Tustin listings or compare nearby restaurants.

12. Burbank Book Festival: best free reader-and-author day

When: October 3, 10 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: readers, local writers, families, romance and young-adult fans, aspiring authors, and visitors who want a free all-day literary program.

Burbank Public Library's third annual festival brings more than 80 authors across adult fiction and nonfiction, romance, children's, middle-grade, and young-adult books to Buena Vista Branch Library. Panels, readings, signings, booksellers, bookish vendors, and the unveiling of a new Bookmobile design give the day more range than a standard author talk. Admission is free; choose two panels or readings before browsing, bring a tote for purchases, and walk, bike, carpool, or use alternative transportation because the city says library parking is limited.

13. Long Beach Open Studio Tour: best free artist-led neighborhood crawl

When: October 3-4, 10-11, 17-18, and 24-25, 1-5 p.m. Best for: art buyers, studio-process fans, cyclists, neighborhood explorers, and visitors who prefer a self-directed afternoon to a single gallery.

More than 120 artists across more than 80 studios open their working spaces over four free weekends, with a different part of Long Beach featured each time: Belmont, Uptown, Downtown, then Eastside. The shifting geography is the advantage—choose the neighborhood that fits your date instead of trying to cover the entire city. Build a route from the organizer's interactive map, limit the first pass to three or four studios, and check individual map notes for accessibility, alley entrances, live music, or workshops. Walking or biking works for tighter clusters; a car is more practical when studios are spread out.

Use the official tour overview and interactive neighborhood maps, confirm the four-weekend schedule and 1-5 p.m. hours, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

The third edition highlights Latino-owned Long Beach restaurants and chefs through limited menus and featured dishes, with current participants spanning coffee, Mexican, Spanish, and waterfront dining. This is the guide's strongest food-specific choice because it turns a directory search into a defined week, but it is not one universal prix-fixe offer: menus, prices, hours, reservations, and service charges vary by restaurant. The City of West Hollywood screens the PG-rated The Princess and the Frog at West Hollywood Park, with pre-movie activities beginning two or more hours before sunset. It is the guide's easiest zero-admission movie plan, but the open lawn format means comfort depends on preparation. Pasadena's 28th annual celebration begins with local schools, community groups, elected officials, dignitaries, neighbors, and friends gathering for a parade from Los Robles Avenue and Buckeye Street, then continues in front of City Hall. The two-stage format is the reason to go: choose the parade for community energy, the festival for food and entertainment, or both for the full five-hour experience. 20. Continuum at Historic Belmar Park: best free music-and-history gathering

When: October 11, noon-5 p.m. Best for: live-music audiences, Black history and culture learners, families, local-history visitors, small-business shoppers, and groups seeking a free all-ages afternoon.

This newly announced Santa Monica program centers Belmar, one of California's oldest African American seaside communities, through KCRW DJs, live hip-hop, R&B, jazz, and string performances, a Black Market Flea vendor village, a Sam Sanders-led history conversation, art-making, archival installations, and interpretive panels around the park. It ranks above a standard concert because the music and market connect directly to place, history, and Black entrepreneurship. Admission is free, but food and shopping need a separate budget; arrive early for the historical panels, then use the stage schedule to choose the performances and conversation that matter most to your group.

21. Long Beach Marathon weekend: best waterfront race-and-spectator plan

The 42nd annual weekend separates its formats clearly: Saturday's Aquarium of the Pacific 5K begins at 6:50 a.m. and can be walked or run, while Sunday begins with the 5:30 a.m. marathon and also includes the beachfront half marathon, bike tour, and wheelchair marathon. The 5K is the most approachable choice; the marathon or half marathon suits trained participants chasing a goal. Registration, road closures, start corrals, and aquarium discounts are event-specific, so confirm the exact product rather than treating the weekend as one interchangeable ticket.

23. Burbank Haunted Adventure: best lower-cost guided Halloween maze

When: October 16, 17, 23, and 24, 7-9:30 p.m. Best for: horror fans ages 6 and older, families with older children, small groups comparing ticketed haunts, and visitors who want a city-run alternative to a large theme-park night.

The City of Burbank turns the Starlight Bowl site into a guided Halloween maze on four nights. Advance tickets cost $10 for adults and $5 for children 12 and younger; walk-up prices rise to $12 and $7, and walk-up admission is not guaranteed. This is the guide's value haunt, but it is physically and emotionally more demanding than Kidspace: children under 6 are not admitted, younger guests need an adult, and the route includes stairs and uphill walking. Buy for the correct date, request ADA accommodation at least a week ahead, and leave props, costumes, and lights at home.

24. Beverly Hills Art Show: best free late-window fine-art weekend

The City of Beverly Hills fills four blocks of Beverly Gardens Park with 240 artists working across painting, sculpture, watercolor, photography, mixed media, ceramics, glass, jewelry, drawing, and printmaking. 25. Pasadena Fall Festival: best free neighborhood Halloween afternoon

When: October 24, 3-6 p.m. Best for: families, costume-contest participants, younger children, carnival-game fans, and Pasadena visitors looking for a compact no-admission Halloween plan.

Pasadena's Parks, Recreation and Community Services team brings costume contests, carnival games, free food, and family activities to Victory Park. 26. Walt Disney Archives and Disneyland art at Muzeo: best flexible Anaheim museum stop

When: Wednesday-Sunday, 11 a.m.-5 p.m., through November 1. Best for: animation fans, artists, families, Disney-history readers, and Anaheim visitors who want a lower-cost indoor culture plan away from the theme parks.

One admission covers three complementary exhibitions: the Walt Disney Archives' world-premiere Capturing Life, Creating Character uses rarely seen photographs, materials, and interactives to explain how live performers informed animated movement; Anaheimland tells Disneyland history through local workers, neighbors, memorabilia, and oral histories; and The Happiest Place: SHAG Imagines Disneyland focuses on the artist's mid-century interpretations of familiar attractions. The combination is the reason to go—start with the animation-process exhibition, then choose local history or design according to your group. General admission is $10 for ages 14 and older, $8 for Anaheim residents, $7 for seniors and children ages 4-13, and $1 for up to four visitors presenting a qualifying EBT card and photo ID. The museum validates parking in Car Parks 3 and 5 and does not permit large bags or strollers in the galleries.

27. Lucas Museum opening in Los Angeles: best new cultural landmark

When: Opens September 22; timed admission is required. Best for: movie lovers, families, visual-storytelling fans, architecture visitors, and anyone choosing one major new Los Angeles museum this fall.
