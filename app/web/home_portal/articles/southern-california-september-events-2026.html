September 2026 event guide

Forty-two Southern California September plans worth building a day around.

Start with Thai Fest by the Beach, Dodgers-Giants, Spider Pavilion, Long Beach's food passport, Coastal Cleanup Day, and Kidspace Halloween, then plan The Huntington's Laura Aguilar exhibition, Long Beach's free Ranchos Walk, Anaheim's free Spinal Dread festival, Glendale Tech Week and film festival, MOLAA's Lucha Libre weekend, the Autry Block Party, museums, sports, food festivals, and design across Southern California. These picks are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Seconds at PCH Food Fest in Long Beach: best flexible waterfront food passport

When: September 17-27. Best for: diners who want several days to participate, waterfront groups, Hispanic Heritage Month audiences, and visitors who prefer a passport format to one fixed tasting menu.

The opening event pairs food and beverage samples along Seaport Way with live music and a 6-9 p.m. salsa band; its enclosed sampling area is 21 and older. The following ten-day restaurant promotion uses a passport, exclusive dishes, specials, and discounts, with completed stamps entering diners for prizes. Choose the kickoff for atmosphere and sampling, or a later date for a lower-pressure meal. Verify each participating restaurant's offer and hours before traveling, and do not assume the 21-and-older rule applies to the full shopping-center promotion.

Use Visit Long Beach's current Food Fest details and organizer links, then compare restaurants among 208 Long Beach listings or the separate Long Beach Burger Week.

2. Halloween at Kidspace in Pasadena: best repeatable young-family fall visit

When: September 18-October 31. Best for: families with younger children, costume lovers, museum members, and groups that want seasonal play without a late-night haunted attraction.

Kidspace layers costume parades at 11 a.m., 1 p.m., and 3 p.m. onto a spooky trike house, Adopt-a-Monster workshop, Witch's Kitchen, and pumpkin bowling. The program is included with museum admission, so it delivers better value as a full Kidspace day than as a quick Halloween photo stop; members receive the seasonal programming with their regular access. Reserve admission for the date you want, check adjusted hours before leaving, and choose Dark Harbor instead if your group wants after-dark scares rather than child-centered play.

Check Kidspace's official program, daily parade times, and tickets, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

3. Thai Fest by the Beach in Santa Monica: best free food-and-culture weekend

When: September 19-20. Best for: families, Thai-food fans, multigenerational groups, and visitors who want a free festival beside the Pier.

The organizer brings Thai street food and modern fusion vendors together with traditional music, dance, Muay Thai demonstrations, Asian American performers, family entertainment, and photo stops. Free admission makes this the weekend's strongest broad-value food festival, but food is sold separately and the Pier setting can become crowded. Arrive earlier for shorter vendor lines, choose a few dishes to share instead of trying every booth, and use transit or a preselected parking structure rather than circling near the waterfront.

Review the official Thai Fest program and free-admission notice, use the ranked Santa Monica September guide for complementary free plans, then browse Santa Monica listings or compare Santa Monica restaurants.

4. Dodgers vs. Giants in Los Angeles: best rivalry-series choice

When: September 18 at 7:15 p.m., September 19 at 6:10 p.m., and September 20 at 1:10 p.m. Best for: baseball fans, rivalry-game groups, families choosing a Sunday matinee, and visitors who want three ticket windows to compare.

The Dodgers return to Dodger Stadium for a three-game weekend against San Francisco after clinching a postseason berth. Friday offers the classic night-game rhythm, Saturday starts slightly earlier, and Sunday is the most family-friendly choice and includes a Decorte trading-card giveaway while supplies last. Compare seat and parking inventory across all three dates, load mobile tickets before reaching the gates, and check the official game page for probable pitchers, promotions, and any time changes.

Check the official Dodgers schedule and live ticket links, review official transportation and parking options, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

5. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together, allow time for the main museum, and note that the museum closes at 3 p.m. on September 19 because of nearby events and road closures.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

6. Long Beach Burger Week: best one-city food crawl

When: Through September 20. Best for: burger fans, mixed-budget groups, and diners who want one focused city with enough options for more than one visit.

The organizer lists more than 20 participating restaurants, food trucks, breweries, bars, and pop-ups, with $5, $10, $15, and $20 menu filters plus a dog-burger option. That makes this the easier of the two restaurant weeks for a compact outing: choose a price first, then a Long Beach neighborhood, and confirm whether the offer is dine-in, takeout, or delivery before traveling. Plant-based diners should use the published menu descriptions instead of assuming every stop has an alternative.

Compare the current Long Beach Burger Week participants and price filters, cross-check the dates with Visit Long Beach's September guide, then browse restaurants among 208 Long Beach listings.

7. California Coastal Cleanup Day: best free volunteer morning

When: September 19, 9 a.m.-noon. Best for: volunteers, families with older children, school and workplace groups, and visitors who want a useful outdoor morning rather than a spectator event.

The California Coastal Commission coordinates the statewide cleanup, while Long Beach and Heal the Bay publish local sites and registration details. Long Beach's city event centers on Alamitos Bay Marina, and the Aquarium of the Pacific lists a separate cleanup at Alamitos Beach; Heal the Bay offers more than 60 Los Angeles County choices. Pick the closest registered site instead of driving across the region, complete any waiver in advance, wear closed-toe shoes, and bring sun protection, water, and reusable cleanup gear when the site requests it.

Start with the California Coastal Commission's official cleanup guide, compare Long Beach's city listing or Heal the Bay's Los Angeles County sites, then browse 208 Long Beach listings for a nearby post-cleanup stop.

8. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

9. Zootoberfest at the L.A. Zoo: best family Oktoberfest mashup

When: September 19-20, 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Best for: families, zoo members, mixed-age groups, and visitors who want seasonal food and live music without giving up a full wildlife day.

The L.A. Zoo's new weekend celebration layers traditional Oktoberfest bands, German-inspired food, seasonal beer, root beer floats, lawn games, keeper talks, a braid bar, and photo installations onto regular zoo admission. The event programming is included with a general-admission ticket or active membership; food and drinks cost extra. This is the better family choice than an adults-first beer hall, but it still works best as a zoo visit: arrive near opening, prioritize animals before they begin moving inside around 4 p.m., and treat the music and food as flexible breaks rather than the whole itinerary.

Review the official Zootoberfest program, compare current admission and arrival guidance, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or nearby restaurant options.

10. Gatsby Redux at Greystone: best site-specific performance

When: September 16, 17, and 19, with arrival from 6-6:30 p.m. Best for: dance and theater audiences, architecture lovers, date nights, and visitors who want a performance shaped by a historic Beverly Hills setting.

Mixed eMotion Theatrix leads the audience through Greystone Mansion's terraced grounds in a movement-driven interpretation inspired by The Great Gatsby. The setting and audience journey make this more distinctive than a conventional seated performance, but they also determine who will enjoy it: guests walk and stand on grass, slate, brick, stairs, and other uneven surfaces. Tickets are $30 for Beverly Hills residents and $35 for non-residents. Choose the September 19 performance if a weekend night matters, wear shoes for the terrain even if you adopt the encouraged 1920s styling, and use the early-arrival window for the city view.

Check the City of Beverly Hills event and ticket details, compare the official visitor overview, then browse Beverly Hills listings or compare Beverly Hills restaurants.

11. San Clemente Car Show: best free downtown vehicle showcase

When: September 20, 10 a.m.-3 p.m. Best for: classic-car fans, families, photographers, downtown shoppers, and groups that want a free Orange County day without a fixed performance schedule.

The San Clemente Downtown Business Association marks the show's thirtieth year with more than 250 classic, vintage, muscle, exotic, woodie, and modern cars along the 100 and 200 blocks of Avenida Del Mar. Live music, food, shopping, and slot-car racing broaden the visit beyond the vehicle rows. Spectator admission is free, but downtown parking is extremely limited; use the free trolley promoted by the organizer, arrive near opening for clearer views, and leave time for the adjacent farmers market and local businesses.

When: Select nights September 18-November 1. Best for: horror fans, thrill-seeking groups, Halloween-season visitors, and people who want mazes, live entertainment, rides, and themed food in one waterfront event.

The Queen Mary and surrounding harbor become a large after-dark festival with haunted mazes, hundreds of performers, immersive attractions, carnival rides, themed bars and lounges, food, and optional VIP experiences. The variety makes this a stronger all-evening choice than a single haunted house, but tickets are sold online and the ship's calendar controls which nights are active. Parking changes on September 18-20: day visitors are directed to the Pike garage with a complimentary shuttle, while rideshare drop-offs remain within walking distance. Buy only after checking the selected night's entry time, ticket tier, age guidance, and attraction availability.

19. Walt Disney Archives and Disneyland art at Muzeo: best flexible Anaheim museum stop

When: Wednesday-Sunday, 11 a.m.-5 p.m., through November 1. Best for: animation fans, artists, families, Disney-history readers, and Anaheim visitors who want a lower-cost indoor culture plan away from the theme parks.

One admission covers three complementary exhibitions: the Walt Disney Archives' world-premiere Capturing Life, Creating Character uses rarely seen photographs, materials, and interactives to explain how live performers informed animated movement; Anaheimland tells Disneyland history through local workers, neighbors, memorabilia, and oral histories; and The Happiest Place: SHAG Imagines Disneyland focuses on the artist's mid-century interpretations of familiar attractions. The combination is the reason to go—start with the animation-process exhibition, then choose local history or design according to your group. General admission is $10 for ages 14 and older, $8 for Anaheim residents, $7 for seniors and children ages 4-13, and $1 for up to four visitors presenting a qualifying EBT card and photo ID. The museum validates parking in Car Parks 3 and 5 and does not permit large bags or strollers in the galleries.
