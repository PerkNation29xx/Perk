September 2026 event guide

Thirty Southern California September plans worth building a day around.

Start with Spider Pavilion, South Coast Repertory's Into the Woods, Pasadena Playhouse's The Visit, Long Beach's final-day food and culture programs, and Kidspace Halloween, then plan the free Los Angeles Korean Festival, Burbank Book Festival, Glendale Cultural Festival, the Watts Towers Jazz Festival, Pasadena's Route 66 exhibition, Glendale's Artifacts from an Unborn Empire exhibition, the Academy Museum's The Horror Show, and more museums, performance, food, film, and design across Southern California. These picks are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Seconds at PCH Food Fest in Long Beach: best flexible waterfront food passport

When: September 17-27. Best for: diners who want several days to participate, waterfront groups, Hispanic Heritage Month audiences, and visitors who prefer a passport format to one fixed tasting menu.

The opening event pairs food and beverage samples along Seaport Way with live music and a 6-9 p.m. salsa band; its enclosed sampling area is 21 and older. The following ten-day restaurant promotion uses a passport, exclusive dishes, specials, and discounts, with completed stamps entering diners for prizes. Choose the kickoff for atmosphere and sampling, or a later date for a lower-pressure meal. Verify each participating restaurant's offer and hours before traveling, and do not assume the 21-and-older rule applies to the full shopping-center promotion.

Use Visit Long Beach's current Food Fest details and organizer links, then compare restaurants among 208 Long Beach listings or plan the Long Beach Urban Farm Dinner.

2. Halloween at Kidspace in Pasadena: best repeatable young-family fall visit

When: September 18-October 31. Best for: families with younger children, costume lovers, museum members, and groups that want seasonal play without a late-night haunted attraction.

Kidspace layers costume parades at 11 a.m., 1 p.m., and 3 p.m. onto a spooky trike house, Adopt-a-Monster workshop, Witch's Kitchen, and pumpkin bowling. The program is included with museum admission, so it delivers better value as a full Kidspace day than as a quick Halloween photo stop; members receive the seasonal programming with their regular access. Reserve admission for the date you want, check adjusted hours before leaving, and choose Dark Harbor instead if your group wants after-dark scares rather than child-centered play.

Check Kidspace's official program, daily parade times, and tickets, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

3. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

4. Into the Woods in Costa Mesa: best full-scale musical-theater choice

When: Through October 18. Best for: Sondheim fans, theater groups, older children, date nights, and visitors who want a substantial indoor performance with several dates to compare.

South Coast Repertory's fortieth-anniversary production runs about two hours and 35 minutes with an intermission and is recommended for ages 10 and older; fog, haze, and strobe effects are used. This is the guide's strongest long-run musical choice because the schedule offers matinees and evenings, plus an actor talk September 23 and a community conversation September 27. Pick a matinee for a family or dining-centered day, check the exact seat price before committing, and allow enough time for the full performance rather than pairing it with another major event.

Compare South Coast Repertory's official dates, tickets, age guidance, and access performance, read the theater's production and parking notes, then browse 34 Costa Mesa listings or compare Costa Mesa restaurants.

5. The Visit at Pasadena Playhouse: best dark-comedy theater choice

When: Through October 4. Best for: theater audiences, dark-comedy fans, date nights, and parents or disabled patrons choosing a specifically supported performance.

Friedrich Dürrenmatt's comedy follows a wealthy woman who offers to rescue her struggling hometown on an impossible condition. Pasadena Playhouse's production runs one hour and 45 minutes without intermission and includes theatrical haze, flashing lights, and prop guns. The September 27 matinee includes open captioning and audio description, making today the guide's most practical theater choice for patrons using those services; later performances continue through October 4. Compare the exact performance and ticket inventory before building dinner around the show.

Use Pasadena Playhouse's official schedule, tickets, runtime, childcare, access, and content guidance, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

6. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

7. Queen Mary's Dark Harbor: best long-running Halloween night

When: Select nights September 18-November 1. Best for: horror fans, thrill-seeking groups, Halloween-season visitors, and people who want mazes, live entertainment, rides, and themed food in one waterfront event.

The Queen Mary and surrounding harbor become a large after-dark festival with haunted mazes, hundreds of performers, immersive attractions, carnival rides, themed bars and lounges, food, and optional VIP experiences. The variety makes this a stronger all-evening choice than a single haunted house, but tickets are sold online and the ship's calendar controls which nights are active. Buy only after checking the selected night's entry time, ticket tier, age guidance, attraction availability, and current parking instructions.

Review the Queen Mary's official fall program, check the current parking and shuttle notice, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

8. Westminster Fall Festival: best free four-day Orange County fair

When: September 24-27. Best for: families, carnival groups, cultural-night visitors, drone-show audiences, and anyone who wants free entry with several distinct days to choose from.

The City of Westminster splits the festival into useful day-by-day formats: Thursday includes Cultural Night and Lotería, Friday adds the opening ceremony and a new drone show, Saturday brings a Moon Lantern Festival, and Sunday features the Maker's Hive market. Admission is free; rides and food cost extra. Friday is the best spectacle choice, Saturday offers the widest family-and-culture mix, and Sunday is the calmer shopping day. Compare discounted ride wristbands before arriving and use the city's schedule rather than assuming every attraction runs all four days.

Review the City of Westminster's official schedule, admission, and ride-ticket details, then browse Westminster listings or compare nearby restaurants.

9. SteelCraft Long Beach Oktoberfest: best free-entry neighborhood celebration

When: September 25-27. Best for: families, live-polka fans, dog owners, casual food groups, and visitors who want an Oktoberfest without a separate admission charge.

SteelCraft builds three different sessions around its food-hall vendors: Friday runs 6-10 p.m. with West Coast Prost and face painting; Saturday runs 2:30-10 p.m. with pop-ups, wiener-dog races, stein contests, a DJ, and Hammerstein; Sunday runs 1-5 p.m. with West Coast Prost and a shorter family-friendly finish. Entry is free, while food, beer, and limited-edition steins are separate. Saturday has the most programming and the largest crowds; Friday or Sunday is the easier choice for a shorter visit.

Check SteelCraft's official three-day schedule and free-entry notice, compare the organizer's contest and family FAQ, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

10. Lucha Libre Weekend at MOLAA: best three-day culture-and-sport crossover

When: September 25-27. Best for: wrestling fans, Latin American art and film audiences, families choosing a Sunday program, and visitors who want one event with clearly different day formats.

MOLAA's 30th-anniversary weekend begins with a Friday book launch and lucha-cinema installation, peaks Saturday with live cumbia and wrestling, then shifts Sunday to a marketplace, workshops, meet-and-greets, panels, and film screenings. Saturday is the best choice for spectacle; Sunday offers the broadest family and museum mix. Individual tickets are available after all-access badges closed, and on-campus parking will be very limited, so buy the specific day in advance and favor rideshare, bus, or walking.

Compare MOLAA's official three-day program, tickets, and arrival guidance, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or find a Long Beach meal.

11. Taiwan Carnival in Anaheim: best free night-market-style culture weekend

When: September 27, noon-8 p.m. Best for: Taiwanese-food fans, families, traditional-arts audiences, marketplace shoppers, and visitors seeking a free Anaheim festival outside the theme parks.

Anaheim GardenWalk's three-day program combines Taiwanese street food, a traditional puppet show, live music and dance, artisan shopping, games, collectible pins, and temple-festival-inspired photo displays. Admission is free; a free advance RSVP adds access to prize drawings and a festival map but should not be confused with paid admission. Friday is the shortest evening visit, Saturday has the longest window, and Sunday is the easiest daytime finish. Budget separately for food and parking: the official listing says the first garage hour is complimentary and later hours are charged, with prepaid full-day options available.

12. Los Angeles Korean Festival: best free four-day culture weekend

When: October 1-4; festival grounds open daily at 10 a.m. Best for: Korean-food fans, live-performance audiences, families, Koreatown visitors, and groups that want a free festival with several days to choose from.

The 53rd annual festival fills Seoul International Park with traditional and contemporary performance, Korean food, cultural exhibits, shopping, family activities, and community booths. Free admission and four full days make this the guide's strongest broad-access October festival, but food, shopping, and parking are separate costs. Choose Thursday or Friday for a lighter first look, or the weekend for the fullest atmosphere; use Metro or the organizer's continuous parking shuttles because the published lots are cashless and neighborhood traffic will be heavy.

Review Discover Los Angeles' current free-admission overview, confirm the organizer's October 1-4 dates and operating hours, check cashless parking and shuttle guidance, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

13. Burbank Book Festival: best free reader-and-author day

When: October 3, 10 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: readers, local writers, families, romance and young-adult fans, aspiring authors, and visitors who want a free all-day literary program.

Burbank Public Library's third annual festival brings more than 80 authors across adult fiction and nonfiction, romance, children's, middle-grade, and young-adult books to Buena Vista Branch Library. Panels, readings, signings, booksellers, bookish vendors, and the unveiling of a new Bookmobile design give the day more range than a standard author talk. Admission is free; choose two panels or readings before browsing, bring a tote for purchases, and walk, bike, carpool, or use alternative transportation because the city says library parking is limited.

Use the City of Burbank's official author count, program overview, admission, and parking advice, then browse 968 Burbank listings or find a nearby Burbank meal.

14. Glendale Cultural Festival: best inaugural downtown culture night

When: October 4, 4-8 p.m. Best for: families, music and dance audiences, global-food explorers, artisan shoppers, and Glendale visitors who prefer a compact evening event.

Glendale's inaugural city festival brings live music, cultural performances, global flavors, art, artisan vendors, and face painting to Artsakh Paseo. The four-hour downtown format is easier to navigate than a sprawling all-day fair, and it pairs naturally with a meal or the nearby Jewel City series. Food and vendor purchases cost extra; confirm any admission or parking update on the city page, arrive early for family activities, and use the performance schedule to decide whether to stay for the full evening.

15. Baja Splash in Long Beach: best bilingual culture-and-aquarium day

When: September 27, 9 a.m.-5 p.m. Best for: families, multigenerational groups, Spanish- and English-speaking visitors, and aquarium guests who want live culture integrated into a full museum day.

The Aquarium of the Pacific's twenty-fifth annual program celebrates cultures from Mexico, Central America, South America, and beyond through traditional dance, live music, bilingual education, and community booths. Eight short live presentations are scheduled between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., which makes it easier to alternate festival programming with galleries instead of waiting in one area all day. The festival is included with timed general admission: $49.95 for adults, $34.95 for children ages 3-11, $46.95 for seniors 62 and older, and free for members with reservations and children under three. This is a better value for visitors who also want a full aquarium visit; choose SteelCraft's free-entry Oktoberfest for a lower-cost Long Beach festival day.

Review the Aquarium's official program, prices, and reservation requirement, use the Spanish event page, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

16. Jane Austen UnScripted in West Hollywood: best free park-theatre plan

When: September 27 at 4 p.m. Best for: improv audiences, Jane Austen readers, picnic groups, and visitors who want a free performance rather than a ticketed theater night.

The City of West Hollywood and Impro Theatre turn audience suggestions into a new Regency-era comedy at Kings Road Park. Today's 90-minute outdoor finale is more flexible than a ticketed theater night and easy to pair with another West Hollywood stop. Admission is free with RSVP; seating is outdoors, so bring a blanket or low chair, arrive early enough to settle in without blocking sightlines, and check the city listing for accessibility and weather updates.

17. Walt Disney Archives and Disneyland art at Muzeo: best flexible Anaheim museum stop

When: Wednesday-Sunday, 11 a.m.-5 p.m., through November 1. Best for: animation fans, artists, families, Disney-history readers, and Anaheim visitors who want a lower-cost indoor culture plan away from the theme parks.

One admission covers three complementary exhibitions: the Walt Disney Archives' world-premiere Capturing Life, Creating Character uses rarely seen photographs, materials, and interactives to explain how live performers informed animated movement; Anaheimland tells Disneyland history through local workers, neighbors, memorabilia, and oral histories; and The Happiest Place: SHAG Imagines Disneyland focuses on the artist's mid-century interpretations of familiar attractions. The combination is the reason to go—start with the animation-process exhibition, then choose local history or design according to your group. General admission is $10 for ages 14 and older, $8 for Anaheim residents, $7 for seniors and children ages 4-13, and $1 for up to four visitors presenting a qualifying EBT card and photo ID. The museum validates parking in Car Parks 3 and 5 and does not permit large bags or strollers in the galleries.

Read Muzeo's official Walt Disney Archives exhibition overview, compare all three current exhibitions, check admission, accessibility, bags, and validated parking, then browse 35 Anaheim listings or compare Anaheim restaurants.

18. Lucas Museum opening in Los Angeles: best new cultural landmark

When: Opens September 22; timed admission is required. Best for: movie lovers, families, visual-storytelling fans, architecture visitors, and anyone choosing one major new Los Angeles museum this fall.

The museum's official opening announcement confirms more than 1,300 works across over 30 galleries, a continuous film program, a free research library, Skywalker Grill, and a new 13-acre park in Exposition Park. Adult admission is $25, visitors 17 and younger enter free, and several other free and reduced-price categories are published on the ticket page. This ranks as the month's strongest new indoor culture pick, but the 100,000 square feet of galleries makes a focused first visit smarter than trying to absorb everything: choose two or three collection themes, reserve a timed slot, and leave time for the building and gardens.

Read the museum's official opening announcement, check current ticket prices and eligibility, then use 61 Los Angeles directory listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

19. Where History Meets the Road in Pasadena: best Route 66 centennial exhibition

When: September 26-January 24, 2027; Thursday-Sunday, noon-5 p.m. Best for: Route 66 travelers, Pasadena-history readers, design and transportation audiences, families with older children, and visitors who prefer a flexible museum date.

Pasadena Museum of History traces how Route 66 and Colorado Boulevard shaped mobility, commerce, roadside culture, architecture, and preservation through photographs, memorabilia, and interpretive displays. General admission is $9, seniors pay $7, and museum members, students, and visitors under 18 enter free. Opening weekend is the most timely choice, but the four-month run makes this a lower-pressure alternative to the crowded Chalk Festival. Allow about 60-90 minutes, check the museum's current hours before leaving, and pair the exhibition with one Colorado Boulevard stop rather than turning the centennial into a cross-city checklist.

Review Visit Pasadena's official dates, admission, hours, and exhibition overview, explore the Route 66 centennial guide, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

Mariachi Tierra Mia reinterprets Smiths favorites across the British band's catalog with leading Los Angeles mariachi artists. 25. Laura Aguilar: Day of the Dead at The Huntington: best long-run photography opening

When: September 20-March 1, 2027; The Huntington is closed Tuesdays. Best for: photography audiences, Chicano-art and Los Angeles-history readers, museum regulars, and Pasadena-area visitors who need a flexible fall date.

The second rotation of The Huntington's Laura Aguilar presentation focuses on a lesser-known early-1990s series documenting Día de los Muertos celebrations in Los Angeles homes and public gatherings, including artists, couples, and families active in the city's Chicano art community. The long run makes this a better choice than a one-weekend event for visitors who want time with both the galleries and gardens. Reserve general admission for a non-Tuesday visit, start with the photography installation while attention is fresh, and leave enough time for the wider campus rather than treating it as a quick standalone stop.

Primal Alchemy's sixteenth annual dinner brings 21 chefs and multiple courses to Rancho Los Alamitos for one moonlit evening centered on local, seasonal, and sustainable ingredients. 28. The Horror Show at the Academy Museum: best new horror-cinema exhibition

When: September 26-July 25, 2027; member preview September 25. Best for: horror-film fans, production-design students, Halloween-season visitors, and museumgoers who want a flexible opening with a long booking window.

The Academy Museum organizes the exhibition as a passage through Gothic, psychological, science, slasher, religion, and ghost themes, using original props, costumes, production material, sound, and immersive sets. The companion Zombies! gallery is designed for all ages, but the main exhibition includes disturbing themes and the museum recommends parental guidance.
