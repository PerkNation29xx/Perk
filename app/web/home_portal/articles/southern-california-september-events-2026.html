November 2026 event guide

Thirty-five Southern California fall plans worth building a day around.

Start with Spider Pavilion, Huntington Beach Oktoberfest, Pasadena's free November 7 Adaptive Sports Festival, November 8 USC Pacific Asia Museum program, and November 15 CicLAvia open-streets day, Glendale's November 7 Nature in Your Neighborhood bird walk, Los Angeles' free November 7 Corita Day Festival, Arcadia's November 7 Community Cleanup Day, Santa Monica's November 7-8 Renegade Craft fair and November 18-January 3 Swiss Winter Village, Long Beach's November 6-22 Little Women and November 21-22 Aquarium Autumn Festival, Burbank's November 6-19 Art Association Fall Show, Anaheim's November 7 Indie Author Day, November 14 Native Art and Acorn Festival, and November 13-January 6 Holidays at the Disneyland Resort, free Veterans Day programs in Santa Monica, Burbank, and Pasadena, West Hollywood's November 14 Next Chapter Writers' Summit and Live On Sunset concert, Beverly Hills' November 19 holiday lighting, the November 20-29 LA Auto Show, and Self Help Graphics' November 7 Día de los Muertos celebration. Flexible museum and exhibition picks extend the guide beyond opening weekend. These recommendations are ranked for timing, practical value, range, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

2. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

3. Pasadena Adaptive Sports Festival: best free try-it-yourself inclusive sports day

When: November 7, 9 a.m.-2 p.m. Best for: adaptive athletes, families, first-time participants, sports fans, and groups looking for a free active day outdoors.

Pasadena and the Triumph Foundation bring the eighth annual festival to Brookside Park with adaptive archery, wheelchair rugby, basketball, pickleball, tennis, and other recreational activities. The hands-on format is the reason to choose this over a spectator-only sports event: visitors can explore several activities in one five-hour window. Admission is free and the event is open to the public. Use the Brookside Park address rather than Pasadena City Hall, arrive early if you want time to try multiple sports, and call the listed city contact before traveling if you need specific equipment or participation accommodations confirmed.

Review the City of Pasadena's official date, hours, Brookside Park location, activity list, and contact information, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

4. CicLAvia Pasadena: best free car-free city exploration day

When: November 15, 9 a.m.-3 p.m. Best for: walkers, cyclists, skaters, families, transit riders, local-business explorers, and visitors who want to experience Pasadena without ordinary street traffic.

CicLAvia returns to Pasadena for the first time in more than a decade, opening portions of Raymond Street and Colorado Boulevard for people to walk, bike, roll, skate, play, and explore. The event is free and non-ticketed, with community hubs, activities, information booths, local programming, and access near Metro's A Line Memorial Park station. It ranks above a conventional street fair because the route itself becomes the experience and connects several business districts. This is not a race: choose only the distance your group can comfortably cover, bring water and sun protection, review the final route and street closures before leaving, and use transit when practical.

Review the City of Pasadena's official hours, route description, and free open-streets format, compare the city's event announcement and transit context, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

When: November 7, noon-4 p.m. Best for: families, art makers, Corita Kent fans, KCRW listeners, Bob Baker Marionette audiences, and visitors looking for a free all-ages Arts District afternoon.

Corita Art Center's seventh annual festival combines interactive art stations, a DJ set by KCRW's Novena Carmel, live music, Bob Baker Marionettes, community-made box-tower murals, food vendors, and early holiday shopping. 6. Arcadia Community Cleanup Day: best make-a-visible-difference morning

When: November 7, 8:30-11 a.m. Best for: neighbors, service groups, garden volunteers, teens and adults seeking a hands-on civic project, and visitors who prefer participation over spectatorship.
