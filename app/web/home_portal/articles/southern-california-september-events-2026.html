November 2026 event guide

Start with Spider Pavilion, the November 12-January 10 NightTales after-dark museum experience, the November 13 opening of the Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center, Pasadena's November 15 CicLAvia open-streets day, The Broad's free November 15 Sensory Morning, Santa Monica's November 11 Veterans Day ceremony and November 18-January 3 Swiss Winter Village, free November 11 Veterans Day programs in Burbank, Pasadena, and Anaheim, Long Beach's November 6-22 Little Women and November 21-22 Aquarium Autumn Festival, Burbank's November 6-19 Art Association Fall Show, Anaheim's November 14 Native Art and Acorn Festival and November 13-January 6 Holidays at the Disneyland Resort, Glendale's November 14 Clothing Swap and Repair Café, West Hollywood's November 14 Next Chapter Writers' Summit and Live On Sunset concert, Beverly Hills' long-run Rodeo Drive sculpture installation and November 19 holiday lighting, and the November 20-29 LA Auto Show. 1. Spider Pavilion at the Natural History Museum: best live-animal fall exhibition

When: September 20-December 6; member preview September 18-19. Best for: families, nature learners, photographers, museum members, and visitors who want a seasonal experience that remains useful beyond one weekend.

The Natural History Museum's open-air pavilion places visitors among hundreds of orb weavers and webs, while a separate den presents tarantulas, wolf spiders, and other species in enclosed habitats. Museum educators add the science context, and the official guide is available in English and Spanish. This is a better choice than a one-day festival for groups that need date flexibility, but the live-animal setting will not suit every visitor. Reserve the pavilion and museum admission together and allow time for the main museum rather than treating the pavilion as a stand-alone stop.

Review the museum's official dates, tickets, live-animal guidance, and early-closing notice, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

2. Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center opening: best new science landmark

When: Opens November 13 at 10 a.m.; daily access continues after opening. Best for: families, spaceflight and engineering fans, school groups, museum travelers, and visitors who want to see Space Shuttle Endeavour in its permanent launch configuration.

The California Science Center's 200,000-square-foot expansion nearly doubles its exhibition space with 100 aerospace artifacts and 100 hands-on exhibits. Its centerpiece is the world's only complete, authentic space-shuttle system displayed vertically in launch position: Endeavour, solid rocket boosters, and the last flight-qualified external tank. This ranks near the top because it is a major permanent opening rather than a one-day program. General museum admission is free, but the Science Center strongly recommends timed reservations for the new galleries because entry without one may be limited; a $4 reservation fee applies to nonmembers.

Reserve through the California Science Center's official ticket page, review the Science Center Foundation's official opening announcement and exhibit details, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

3. CicLAvia Pasadena: best free car-free city exploration day

When: November 15, 9 a.m.-3 p.m. Best for: walkers, cyclists, skaters, families, transit riders, local-business explorers, and visitors who want to experience Pasadena without ordinary street traffic.

CicLAvia returns to Pasadena for the first time in more than a decade, opening portions of Raymond Street and Colorado Boulevard for people to walk, bike, roll, skate, play, and explore. The event is free and non-ticketed, with community hubs, activities, information booths, local programming, and access near Metro's A Line Memorial Park station. It ranks above a conventional street fair because the route itself becomes the experience and connects several business districts. This is not a race: choose only the distance your group can comfortably cover, bring water and sun protection, review the final route and street closures before leaving, and use transit when practical.

Review the City of Pasadena's official hours, route description, and free open-streets format, compare the city's event announcement and transit context, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

4. Sensory Morning at The Broad: best free low-crowd museum visit

When: November 15, 9-10 a.m. Best for: visitors of all ages who benefit from smaller crowds, quieter galleries, adjusted sound and lighting, sensory supports, and advance preparation.

The Broad opens its third-floor collection galleries and Yayoi Kusama's Infinity Mirrored Room—The Souls of Millions of Light Years Away before public hours for a sensory-friendly visit. The free event includes reduced crowd levels, sound and lighting adjustments, a preparation narrative, and a limited number of sensory kits with noise-reducing headphones and tactile items. It ranks above a standard museum morning because the access accommodations are the program, not an add-on. Reservations are required and capacity is limited, so secure a ticket before building the rest of the day around downtown Los Angeles.

Review The Broad's official date, free-ticket requirement, gallery access, sensory supports, and accommodation contact, then browse 61 Los Angeles listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

When: November 6-19; opening reception November 6, 7-9 p.m. Best for: local-art supporters, gallery newcomers, Burbank residents, artists seeking community, and groups that need a flexible indoor November stop.

The Burbank Art Association brings its annual fall show to the Betsy Lueke Creative Arts Center, with music and light refreshments at the Friday opening reception and gallery access during the center's regular hours through November 19. The long run makes this easier to fit than a one-night festival, while the opening is the stronger choice for conversation and community atmosphere. The city page does not list a ticket price, so confirm admission before leaving; if you visit after opening night, check the center's weekday and Saturday hours because it is not open on a standard seven-day museum schedule.

6. NightTales at the Natural History Museum: best after-dark science-and-light experience

When: Select nights November 12-January 10, 2027. Best for: museum regulars, date nights, families with older children, light-art audiences, natural-history fans, and visitors who want an evening alternative to a daytime gallery visit.

NightTales transforms selected museum and garden spaces with glowing installations, cinematic projections, sound, and illuminated interpretations of real specimens from the Natural History Museum's collections. It earns a high rank because it combines art, science, and technology in a new evening format rather than simply extending normal museum hours. The event does not open the full museum, and dates, entry windows, parking, and prices vary, so choose a specific session before building dinner or transit plans around it. Visitors sensitive to bright or changing light and amplified sound should review access details with the museum before booking.

When: November 6-22; evening and matinee performances. Best for: families with older children, Louisa May Alcott readers, musical-theater fans, school groups, and visitors who want several dates and times to compare.

When: November 11, 11 a.m.-1 p.m. Best for: veterans, military families, civic-program audiences, live-music listeners, aviation enthusiasts, and groups seeking a free public remembrance.

Santa Monica and the U.S. Army move the city's seventh annual ceremony to Beach Parking Lot 4 between Santa Monica Beach and Crescent Bay Park. The 11 a.m.-noon ceremony includes military and city speakers, the 300th Army Band, a future-soldier swearing-in, and a planned Chinook/C-130 flyover; music and entertainment continue until 1 p.m. It ranks as a strong free civic event because the beachside setting and full program are distinctive, but aircraft participation can change with operations or weather. Arrive before 11 a.m., bring a coastal layer, expect amplified sound, and confirm parking or transit before traveling.

19. Where History Meets the Road in Pasadena: best Route 66 centennial exhibition

When: September 26-January 24, 2027; Thursday-Sunday, noon-5 p.m. Best for: Route 66 travelers, Pasadena-history readers, design and transportation audiences, families with older children, and visitors who prefer a flexible museum date.

Pasadena Museum of History traces how Route 66 and Colorado Boulevard shaped mobility, commerce, roadside culture, architecture, and preservation through photographs, memorabilia, and interpretive displays. 20. Portraits: Faces and Waves in Santa Monica: best free flexible photography stop

When: October 3-March 27, 2027; daily except special events. Best for: photography audiences, Pier visitors, coastal-history readers, portraiture students, and groups needing a quieter free alternative to a timed festival.

The Annenberg Community Beach House Gallery pairs Sam Comen's portraits of 274 Santa Monica Pier visitors with Anthony Friedkin's photographs of waves made near the Beach House and State Beach. The local focus gives the exhibition more context than a generic gallery stop: one body of work records who came to the Pier during America's 250th year, while the other treats individual waves as short-lived forms. Admission is free, but gallery access can change around special events. Check same-day hours, allow 30-60 minutes, and combine the show with the beach or one nearby meal rather than expecting a full museum campus.
