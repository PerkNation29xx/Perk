September 2026 event guide

Thirty-six Southern California September plans worth building a day around.

Start with a hands-on maker weekend, Fiestas Patrias celebrations in Pasadena and Santa Ana, Dana Point maritime history, Huntington Beach Oktoberfest, Fountain Valley's Original Lobster Festival, or Long Beach's free California Turkish Festival, then look ahead to fan culture, Indigenous and Latin American coastal culture, Disney animation art, site-specific dance, Armenian film, Greek food and dance, stadium nights, outdoor art, a major museum opening, creative careers, community wellness, restaurant weeks, local tastings, and a walkable design festival. These picks are ranked for their range, practical value, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Maker Faire Orange County: best hands-on invention weekend

When: September 12, 10 a.m.-5 p.m., and September 13, 10 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: families, students, robotics fans, artists, crafters, tinkerers, and visitors who learn best by trying things.

The organizer's current exhibit roster spans robotics, 3D printing, electronics, engineering, art, crafts, science, sustainability, photography, fashion, and young-maker projects. That breadth makes this the strongest hands-on learning pick in the guide. The venue lists $20 single-day admission, $35 for the weekend, free entry for children four and younger, and $15 parking; choose one day unless your group has enough priority demos and workshops to justify returning.

Check the official Maker Faire tickets and hours, review the current makers and exhibits, then browse 34 Costa Mesa listings for food and practical stops.

2. BlizzCon in Anaheim: best fan and gaming weekend

When: September 12-13; on-site registration begins September 11. Best for: Blizzard fans, esports viewers, cosplayers, collectors, and game-industry job seekers.

The official event page confirms esports finals, Community Night, the Darkmoon Faire, portfolio and résumé reviews, and a Sunday closing performance by LE SSERAFIM. This is the guide's highest-commitment convention pick, so secure admission first and then decide whether to collect credentials Friday. The organizer publishes detailed parking, rideshare, public-transit, and accessibility guidance; review it before booking a hotel or committing to drive both days.

Plan with the official BlizzCon event page, then browse 35 Anaheim listings for nearby food, hotels, shopping, and services.

3. Santa Ana Fiestas Patrias: best free cultural street festival

When: September 12, noon-11 p.m., and September 13, noon-10 p.m.; the Sunday parade starts at 11 a.m. Best for: families, multigenerational groups, live-music fans, and visitors looking for a large free celebration of Mexican independence and Southern California's Hispanic and Latino communities.

The City of Santa Ana's newly published program makes this the guide's strongest free culture pick for the immediate weekend: Saturday centers the 7 p.m. El Grito de Independencia ceremony, while Sunday's Main Street parade leads into a second day of music, artisan and community vendors, food, carnival rides, and cultural activities. Admission is free; the beer garden requires a $5 wristband, valid ID, and a separate drink purchase. Choose Saturday for the ceremony and late-night headline set, or Sunday for the parade and a slightly earlier finish. Check the city's event map and street-closure updates before driving downtown.

Use the City of Santa Ana's official schedule and access updates, compare the official tourism overview, then browse 31 Santa Ana listings or compare Santa Ana restaurants.

4. Dana Point Maritime Festival: best hands-on coastal-history weekend

When: September 11-13; the main Saturday-Sunday program runs 9 a.m.-5 p.m. Best for: families, tall-ship fans, maritime-history learners, and groups that want both free harbor activities and optional ticketed experiences.

The Ocean Institute's forty-second annual festival combines visiting tall ships, sailor reenactors, marine life, pirate and mermaid programs, local vendors, food trucks, live music, and beer gardens. Free Saturday-Sunday areas include Peddler's Village, live music, a sailor camp, and pirate shows; the $20 adult weekend Adventure Pass adds the Ocean Institute campus, ship tours, touch tanks, animal encounters, and hands-on history activities, with lower published prices for seniors and children. Pay for the pass only if your group will use the indoor exhibits and ship access. There is no onsite festival parking, so build the free Dana Point trolley into the plan.

Check the Ocean Institute's official festival program, review the Adventure Pass inclusions and current prices, then browse 14 Dana Point listings or compare Dana Point restaurants.

5. Original Lobster Festival in Fountain Valley: best seafood-and-carnival weekend

When: September 11, 5-10 p.m.; September 12, noon-10 p.m.; and September 13, noon-8 p.m. Best for: seafood fans, families, mixed-interest groups, and visitors who want a meal, live entertainment, and carnival activities in one place.

The thirtieth annual festival combines fresh Maine lobster, an international food court, live music, dance lessons, a sports tent, local vendors, magic, face painting, and carnival rides at Fountain Valley Sports Park. Friday is the value play at $5 admission; Saturday or Sunday general admission is $15 online or $18 at the gate, while children 12 and younger enter free. Food, drinks, rides, and games cost extra. The $40 online lobster-feast bundle is the most predictable choice for visitors coming specifically for the signature meal because it includes admission and guarantees a 1.25-pound lobster; otherwise, buy entry only and compare vendors inside. Parking is free, but arrive before the dinner rush if seating and shorter food lines matter.

Review the official festival program, parking, and family activities, compare the current admission and meal packages, then browse 12 Fountain Valley listings or compare Fountain Valley restaurants.

6. Segerstrom Center Ruby Jubilee: best free all-arts sampler

When: September 13, 2-8 p.m. Best for: families, first-time arts visitors, mixed-interest groups, and anyone choosing one free Costa Mesa event with music, dance, theater, visual art, and hands-on activities.

Segerstrom Center's fortieth-anniversary celebration opens the full campus for a free afternoon of performances, ballet masterclasses, organ demonstrations, an instrument petting zoo, participatory dance, artmaking, venue exploration, and a sensory-friendly program from Studio D. From 6-8 p.m., the format shifts to a 1980s-themed birthday program with food and drinks, The M80s, and a closing drone show. The breadth is the reason to go, but it rewards a shortlist: choose two indoor programs and one plaza activity before arrival, then stay for the evening only if the retro concert and drone show suit the group.

Review the official Costa Mesa tourism program and times, then browse 34 Costa Mesa listings or compare Costa Mesa restaurants.

7. Pasadena Fiestas Patrias: best same-day community celebration

When: September 11, 5-9 p.m. Best for: Pasadena families, multigenerational groups, Latin American culture and music fans, and anyone making a spontaneous Friday-evening plan.

The City of Pasadena's Parks, Recreation and Community Services Department is marking the independence of 18 Latin American countries at Villa Parke Community Center. The four-hour program includes Nueva Dimension Norteña, traditional dance, arts and crafts, food, and family activities. This is the guide's most time-sensitive pick: choose it for a compact neighborhood celebration rather than the larger two-day Santa Ana festival, and arrive early enough to see the family programming before the evening becomes music-led. The city page does not publish an admission price, parking plan, or food prices, so confirm any last-minute access update and carry a separate food budget.

Check the City of Pasadena's event page, then browse 563 Pasadena listings or compare Pasadena restaurants.

8. Huntington Beach Oktoberfest: best repeatable fall festival

When: September 12-November 8, Wednesday-Sunday. Best for: German-food fans, live-band and dance audiences, families choosing a daytime session, and adults looking for a later 21-and-older night.

Old World Village turns one event into several distinct formats: Wednesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday daytime are all ages with free advance admission or $5 at the door; Friday and Saturday evenings are 21 and older with advance admission from $10-$30; and Sundays are all ages at $10 online, $15 at the door, free after 5 p.m., and free for children 12 and younger. German bands, dancers, food, beer, games, and contests run throughout the season. Saturday's opening day is the best first look for families, while a free-in-advance midweek session is the value choice. Parking is free, seating is first come, and the clear-bag policy is strict—check the exact session before choosing tickets.

Compare the official schedule and admission formats, review the age, bag, and entry rules, then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings or compare Huntington Beach restaurants.

9. Gatsby Redux at Greystone: best site-specific performance

When: September 16, 17, and 19, with arrival from 6-6:30 p.m. Best for: dance and theater audiences, architecture lovers, date nights, and visitors who want a performance shaped by a historic Beverly Hills setting.

Mixed eMotion Theatrix leads the audience through Greystone Mansion's terraced grounds in a movement-driven interpretation inspired by The Great Gatsby. The setting and audience journey make this more distinctive than a conventional seated performance, but they also determine who will enjoy it: guests walk and stand on grass, slate, brick, stairs, and other uneven surfaces. Tickets are $30 for Beverly Hills residents and $35 for non-residents. Choose the September 19 performance if a weekend night matters, wear shoes for the terrain even if you adopt the encouraged 1920s styling, and use the early-arrival window for the city view.

Check the City of Beverly Hills event and ticket details, compare the official visitor overview, then browse Beverly Hills listings or compare Beverly Hills restaurants.

10. Baja Splash in Long Beach: best bilingual culture-and-aquarium day

When: September 26-27, 9 a.m.-5 p.m. Best for: families, multigenerational groups, Spanish- and English-speaking visitors, and aquarium guests who want live culture integrated into a full museum day.

The Aquarium of the Pacific's twenty-fifth annual program celebrates cultures from Mexico, Central America, South America, and beyond through traditional dance, live music, bilingual education, and community booths. Eight short live presentations are scheduled between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., which makes it easier to alternate festival programming with galleries instead of waiting in one area all day. The festival is included with timed general admission: $49.95 for adults, $34.95 for children ages 3-11, $46.95 for seniors 62 and older, and free for members with reservations and children under three. This is a better value for visitors who also want a full aquarium visit; for a free waterfront cultural day, choose the September 13 Turkish Festival instead.

Review the Aquarium's official program, prices, and reservation requirement, use the Spanish event page, then browse 208 Long Beach listings or compare Long Beach restaurants.

13. Walt Disney Archives and Disneyland art at Muzeo: best flexible Anaheim museum stop

When: Wednesday-Sunday, 11 a.m.-5 p.m., through November 1. Best for: animation fans, artists, families, Disney-history readers, and Anaheim visitors who want a lower-cost indoor culture plan away from the theme parks.

One admission covers three complementary exhibitions: the Walt Disney Archives' world-premiere Capturing Life, Creating Character uses rarely seen photographs, materials, and interactives to explain how live performers informed animated movement; Anaheimland tells Disneyland history through local workers, neighbors, memorabilia, and oral histories; and The Happiest Place: SHAG Imagines Disneyland focuses on the artist's mid-century interpretations of familiar attractions. The combination is the reason to go—start with the animation-process exhibition, then choose local history or design according to your group. General admission is $10 for ages 14 and older, $8 for Anaheim residents, $7 for seniors and children ages 4-13, and $1 for up to four visitors presenting a qualifying EBT card and photo ID. The museum validates parking in Car Parks 3 and 5 and does not permit large bags or strollers in the galleries.

Read Muzeo's official Walt Disney Archives exhibition overview, compare all three current exhibitions, check admission, accessibility, bags, and validated parking, then browse 35 Anaheim listings or compare Anaheim restaurants.

This ranks as the month's strongest new indoor culture pick, but the 100,000 square feet of galleries makes a focused first visit smarter than trying to absorb everything: choose two or three collection themes, reserve a timed slot, and leave time for the building and gardens.

How to choose quickly

  • Immediate culture: choose Pasadena Fiestas Patrias for a compact Friday evening, Santa Ana Fiestas Patrias for a larger two-day street celebration, the California Turkish Festival for a free Long Beach waterfront visit, or Segerstrom's Ruby Jubilee for a free campus-wide arts sampler.
  • Fan and maker weekend: choose BlizzCon for the September 12-13 gaming commitment or Maker Faire Orange County for hands-on invention; for football, choose UCLA at the Rose Bowl.
