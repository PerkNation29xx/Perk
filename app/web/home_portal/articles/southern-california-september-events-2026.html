September 2026 event guide

Twenty-five Southern California September plans worth building a day around.

Start with today's final Greek-festival and chess sessions, then look ahead to hands-on invention, fan culture, Armenian film, Greek food and dance, stadium nights, outdoor art, a major museum opening, creative careers, free music, community wellness, restaurant weeks, local tastings, and a walkable design festival. These picks are ranked for their range, practical value, and connection to cities with useful Perk Nation directory coverage.

1. Long Beach Greek Festival: best final-day food-and-culture plan

When: Final day today, September 7, opening at noon. Best for: families and food-focused groups looking for a lower-cost festival with music, dancing, and cultural programming.

Visit Long Beach lists $5 admission at Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary Greek Orthodox Church, plus free parking and continuous shuttle service from the nearby VA Medical Center lot. That published shuttle plan gives this festival an edge over events where parking is left to chance. Arrive near opening for a more relaxed meal, or later if live dancing and a busier festival atmosphere matter more to your group.

2. Southern California Open in Glendale: best final-day strategy event

When: Final day today, September 7; the published player-registration deadline has passed. Best for: already-registered players, ambitious juniors and their families, and spectators who confirm access with the organizer.

The City of Glendale says the four-day tournament returns with seven competitive sections, a separate September 7 blitz tournament, and a guaranteed $60,000 main-event prize fund. The range from U1300 through Open is the reason this makes the list, but this is no longer a last-minute player recommendation. If you are not registered, verify spectator access or any organizer-approved late option before traveling; registered players should confirm round times and plan meals around long game windows at the Hilton Glendale.

Use the official Southern California Open guide, then use 100 Glendale directory listings for food, pharmacies, shopping, and other practical stops.

3. Maker Faire Orange County: best hands-on invention weekend

When: September 12, 10 a.m.-5 p.m., and September 13, 10 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: families, students, robotics fans, artists, crafters, tinkerers, and visitors who learn best by trying things.

The organizer's current exhibit roster spans robotics, 3D printing, electronics, engineering, art, crafts, science, sustainability, photography, fashion, and young-maker projects. That breadth makes this the strongest hands-on learning pick in the guide. The venue lists $20 single-day admission, $35 for the weekend, free entry for children four and younger, and $15 parking; choose one day unless your group has enough priority demos and workshops to justify returning.

Check the official Maker Faire tickets and hours, review the current makers and exhibits, then browse 34 Costa Mesa listings for food and practical stops.

4. BlizzCon in Anaheim: best fan and gaming weekend

When: September 12-13; on-site registration begins September 11. Best for: Blizzard fans, esports viewers, cosplayers, collectors, and game-industry job seekers.

The official event page confirms esports finals, Community Night, the Darkmoon Faire, portfolio and résumé reviews, and a Sunday closing performance by LE SSERAFIM. This is the guide's highest-commitment convention pick, so secure admission first and then decide whether to collect credentials Friday. The organizer publishes detailed parking, rideshare, public-transit, and accessibility guidance; review it before booking a hotel or committing to drive both days.

Plan with the official BlizzCon event page, then browse 35 Anaheim listings for nearby food, hotels, shopping, and services.

5. Pasadena ARTWalk: best free art-and-maker weekend

When: September 19-20, 11 a.m.-6 p.m. Best for: art shoppers, design-minded visitors, families, and anyone who wants a free daytime event.

Visit Pasadena lists more than 180 artisans and makers on Green Street between Los Robles and El Molino. The free admission and compact Playhouse Village footprint make it easy to browse without treating the event like an all-day endurance test. Start with a shortlist—visual art, photography, jewelry, fashion, or home goods—then leave a meal break instead of trying to see every booth in one pass.

Confirm details with Visit Pasadena, then use 563 Pasadena listings for coffee, lunch, dinner, or services nearby.

6. Ocean Way Festival in Santa Monica: best destination beach concert

When: September 26-27. Best for: music fans who want a full beach-festival weekend rather than a short evening concert.

The City of Santa Monica confirms The Killers and Olivia Dean as headliners for the inaugural two-day event, with Jack White, Khruangbin, Sublime, Durand Jones and the Indications, Alvvays, and more on the announced lineup. There is no onsite parking, special event rates apply in several city lots, and the bike path is rerouted around the footprint. Use transit or a designated offsite plan and treat each day as a destination outing.

Review the city's current Ocean Way access plan, then browse Santa Monica businesses.

7. Lucas Museum opening in Los Angeles: best new cultural landmark

When: Opens September 22; timed admission is required. Best for: movie lovers, families, visual-storytelling fans, architecture visitors, and anyone choosing one major new Los Angeles museum this fall.

The museum's official opening announcement confirms more than 1,300 works across over 30 galleries, a continuous film program, a free research library, Skywalker Grill, and a new 13-acre park in Exposition Park. Adult admission is $25, visitors 17 and younger enter free, and several other free and reduced-price categories are published on the ticket page. This ranks as the month's strongest new indoor culture pick, but the 100,000 square feet of galleries makes a focused first visit smarter than trying to absorb everything: choose two or three collection themes, reserve a timed slot, and leave time for the building and gardens.

Read the museum's official opening announcement, check current ticket prices and eligibility, then use 61 Los Angeles directory listings or compare Los Angeles restaurants.

8. UCLA's Rose Bowl home opener: best local college-football day

When: September 12. Best for: Bruins fans, alumni groups, and visitors who want a stadium event with time for a Pasadena meal before or after.

UCLA's official 2026 schedule lists San Diego State at the Rose Bowl. This is easier to enjoy when the transportation plan comes before the restaurant plan: use current team and stadium updates for kickoff, parking, shuttle, and entry rules, then choose food around the route you are actually taking rather than the neighborhood you hope will be convenient.

Check UCLA's official football schedule, then start with the Pasadena directory.

9. Craft Beer LB Fest: best local-only beer-and-food afternoon

When: September 12, 1-5 p.m. Best for: craft-beer fans, friend groups, and families who want a compact event built around Long Beach makers rather than a statewide tasting hall.

Visit Long Beach identifies this as the city's only festival focused specifically on Long Beach breweries, food, and artists, and says children are welcome. The posted roster includes Beachwood, Belmont, ISM, Long Beach Beer Lab, Trademark, and other local brewers, plus barbecue, lumpia, and plant-based food vendors; proceeds benefit Rancho Los Cerritos. That local-only format is the reason it outranks a generic tasting event. Adults planning to drink should arrange a designated driver or rideshare, and families should confirm current youth-entry and ticket terms before traveling.

Check Visit Long Beach's current festival listing and tickets, then browse restaurants among 208 Long Beach listings.

10. Armenian Film Festival in Glendale: best five-day cinema program

When: September 16-20. Best for: film lovers, Armenian-culture audiences, filmmakers, students, and visitors who prefer a focused screening or conversation to an all-day convention.

Glendale's official calendar lays out an opening-night rooftop gathering followed by U.S. premieres, filmmaker conversations, short-film programs, and a closing day across several city venues. The breadth is the draw, but the program is not one continuous drop-in event: choose the film or discussion that matters most, confirm its venue and ticket terms, and leave travel time between locations. Opening night emphasizes community and networking rather than a screening.

Start with Glendale's official festival opening listing, compare the remaining festival titles in the city's September calendar, then use 100 Glendale listings for food and practical stops.

11. Burbank Career Transitions Expo: best creative-industry opportunity

When: September 18, 12:30-4:30 p.m. Best for: media and entertainment professionals navigating a career change or looking for focused networking and practical guidance.

The City of Burbank says this is the first event in a new three-part Media Industry Expo series and that the smaller format is designed for more personalized networking, panels, and workshops. That makes it a better fit for people with a defined career question than a broad entertainment festival. Review participating employers and sessions before registering, then arrive with a short introduction and a specific next-step goal.

Read the City of Burbank announcement, then explore 968 Burbank directory listings.

12. Burbank Autumn Arts Festival: best free neighborhood arts afternoon

When: September 19, 4-7 p.m. Best for: families, local-art supporters, casual live-music fans, and groups looking for a free event with a short, manageable time window.

The City of Burbank confirms a free Johnny Carson Park program with a live concert, booths from local artists and arts organizations, and food trucks. Hometown rock band The Velvonics headlines, with more music still to be announced. The compact three-hour format is the strength: arrive early for the art booths, eat when food-truck lines are shorter, and stay for the headliner. No ticket is required, though the city encourages an RSVP; street parking and The Pointe Parking are the published options.

Read the City of Burbank announcement, then browse 968 Burbank listings or compare local restaurants.

13. Pasadena Greek Festival: best later-month food-and-dance weekend

When: September 18-20. Best for: families and mixed-age groups choosing Greek food, traditional music, dance, and a community festival after Labor Day.

The festival organizer confirms the three-day run at Saint Anthony Greek Orthodox Church, with food, traditional music, dance performances, free offsite parking, and shuttle buses about every 10 minutes. This is the more flexible later-month culture pick, but entry is single-use rather than re-entry: plan the meal and performance block you want before arriving. Parking-gate guidance differs between the organizer and tourism listings, so use the organizer's current page on the day of the visit.

Check the official Pasadena Greek Festival schedule and parking, review the current ticket and re-entry policy, then browse 563 Pasadena listings.

14. Pasadena Chalk Festival: best live-art spectacle

When: September 26-27, 10 a.m.-7 p.m. Best for: families, photographers, art students, and visitors who want to watch large murals develop in real time.

Visit Pasadena currently lists more than 500 chalk artists creating 200 murals at Brookside Park, plus a children's chalk area, food trucks, a DJ, and a silent auction that supports arts education. Admission and Rose Bowl Lot I parking are listed as free, which makes this the strongest large-scale value pick in the second half of the month. Arrive earlier for cleaner views of works in progress, then return late enough to compare finished murals.

Check the current Visit Pasadena festival guide, then browse 563 Pasadena listings for a meal or practical stop.

15. Arcadia Health Fair: best practical wellness stop

When: September 12, 9 a.m.-noon. Best for: adults and families looking for free screenings, medical information, and a focused morning plan.

The City of Arcadia lists free flu shots while supplies last, doctor consultations, medical-information booths, and screenings that may include glucose, cholesterol, dental, body-fat, mammogram, menopause, and diabetic-retinopathy services. Some screenings require advance registration, so this is a plan to organize before Saturday morning rather than improvise at the door. Prioritize the screening you need most and treat everything else as a bonus.

Read the City of Arcadia health-fair notice, then use 40 Arcadia directory listings for pharmacies, food, and nearby services.

16. Americana in the Park: best free all-ages concert afternoon

When: September 13, 1:30-6:30 p.m. Best for: families, roots-music fans, and picnic groups looking for a free alternative to a ticketed beach festival.

Santa Monica Cultural Affairs and McCabe's Guitar Shop bring Albert Lee, Ricardo Lemvo & Makina Loca, Abby Posner & the Big Fall, Jason Luckett, and the Bob Baker Marionettes to Gandara Park, with food trucks and children's activities. Pack shade as well as a blanket, and use Big Blue Bus Route 43 or the nearby Metro E Line if that is easier than managing event parking.

Use Santa Monica's official event listing, then search Santa Monica on Perk Nation.

17. Arcadia Mid-Autumn Moon Festival: best free cultural evening

When: September 26, 5-8:30 p.m. Best for: families, multigenerational groups, first-time festivalgoers, and anyone choosing a free San Gabriel Valley evening.

Arcadia's September city newsletter confirms live entertainment, children's activities, vendor booths, food, and mooncake making on the City Hall Lawn. The free admission, compact evening window, and mix of performance and hands-on activity make this a stronger family value than a ticketed night out. Bring blankets or chairs for the performances, eat before the busiest part of the evening if lines matter to your group, and use the city's published parking at City Hall or Santa Anita Park Gate 5.

Read Arcadia's official September festival notice, then browse 40 Arcadia listings or compare local restaurants.

18. Tongva Twilight in Santa Monica: best free picnic-and-movie night

When: Saturday evenings through September 12, beginning at 7 p.m. Best for: a low-cost date, family picnic, or relaxed outdoor evening near the coast.

The City of Santa Monica describes the six-week Tongva Park series as free live music and open-air cinema. It is the strongest budget alternative to Ocean Way: bring a picnic, plan for cooler evening temperatures, and check the city's calendar for the selected Saturday's program. Because it starts at 7 p.m., it can follow a beach afternoon without turning the entire day into a ticketed festival commitment.

Read the city's Tongva Twilight announcement, then find nearby Santa Monica options.

When: September 11-12. Best for: concertgoers who want a major headliner and a fireworks finale in one reserved-seat evening.

The LA Phil lists OneRepublic for the Hollywood Bowl's season-ending fireworks performances, with gates at 6 p.m. and the concert at 8 p.m. This ranks below the free community events on value, but higher on production scale and the simplicity of one clear nighttime plan. Use the Bowl's current venue rules, and decide on shuttle or parking before making dinner reservations.

20. Jewel City Concert Series in Glendale: best free fall music series

When: Saturdays September 26-October 24 at 5 p.m. Best for: families, casual music fans, downtown dinner groups, and visitors who want a short free performance instead of an all-night concert.

The City of Glendale confirms five free, all-ages performances on Artsakh Paseo, beginning with Armenian ensemble String Harmonies on September 26 and continuing with western swing, bolero, retro-pop, and reggae-Latin-funk programs in October. Each set runs about 60 minutes without intermission. Bring a chair because seating is not provided, arrive early enough to settle in, and pair the compact show with one nearby meal rather than overbuilding the evening.

Use the official city arts lineup and visitor guidance, then browse restaurants among 100 Glendale listings.

21. Long Beach Burger Week: best one-city food crawl

When: September 13-20. Best for: burger fans, mixed-budget groups, and diners who want one focused city with enough options for more than one visit.

The organizer lists more than 20 participating restaurants, food trucks, breweries, bars, and pop-ups, with $5, $10, $15, and $20 menu filters plus a dog-burger option. That makes this the easier of the two restaurant weeks for a compact outing: choose a price first, then a Long Beach neighborhood, and confirm whether the offer is dine-in, takeout, or delivery before traveling. Plant-based diners should use the published menu descriptions instead of assuming every stop has an alternative.

Compare the current Long Beach Burger Week participants and price filters, cross-check the dates with Visit Long Beach's September guide, then browse restaurants among 208 Long Beach listings.

22. Orange County Burger Week: best build-your-own dining tour

When: September 20-26. Best for: diners comparing classic, chicken, turkey, mushroom, and plant-based burgers across several Orange County cities.

The official guide organizes menus at $10, $15, $20, and $25, with optional luxury pairings, and its current roster spans Newport Beach, Santa Ana, Irvine, Huntington Beach, Costa Mesa, Orange, Laguna Beach, Fullerton, Dana Point, San Clemente, Fountain Valley, and more. The range is the advantage, but it also creates the main planning trap: do not zigzag across the county for a checklist. Pick one city or adjacent pair, verify that the menu is posted, and make a reservation when the restaurant accepts one; the organizer notes that participants can change.

Start with the official Burger Week price filters, then compare current Perk Nation options in Orange, Huntington Beach, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, or Irvine.

23. Taste of Arcadia: best local dining-and-business sampler

When: September 19, 2-6 p.m.; VIP entry begins at 1 p.m. Best for: adults who want food and beverage sampling, local-business discovery, live entertainment, and one ticketed San Gabriel Valley outing.

Arcadia's September city newsletter identifies food and beverage samples, local businesses, live music, photo booths, and a car show at Santa Anita Park's Paddock Gardens, while the Chamber organizer confirms tickets are on sale and that the event is 21 and over. The focused city connection makes this more useful than a generic tasting expo. Review the current vendor roster and admission tier before buying, use a designated driver or rideshare if sampling alcohol, and leave the rest of the afternoon open rather than stacking another meal.

Read Arcadia's official September event notice, check current tickets and participants with the Chamber organizer, then compare restaurants among 40 Arcadia listings.

24. The Great Outdoors at Greystone: best Beverly Hills nature-and-design stop

When: September 19. Best for: garden lovers, architecture fans, and visitors who prefer a focused daytime cultural plan to a large street festival.

The City of Beverly Hills describes this as its annual celebration of nature at Greystone, running 11 a.m.-4 p.m. with free admission and no advance registration. The setting—not scale—is the reason to go: pair the event with time in the grounds and build the rest of the day around a nearby meal or art walk. The full schedule is due in early September, so check the city page again before arriving.

Confirm the Greystone program with the city, then browse Beverly Hills businesses.

25. Design West Hollywood: best design-industry deep dive

When: September 29-October 1. Best for: interior designers, architects, hospitality professionals, design students, and visitors who want a walkable program rather than one convention hall.

Visit West Hollywood publishes three days of talks, showroom sessions, book events, and evening gatherings across the Design District. The September 30 program is the strongest single-day choice because it runs from a morning styling session through panels on photography, film, Los Angeles design, and a closing cocktail event. Sessions can reach capacity and ticket types vary, so reserve the specific day you want before building a restaurant or hotel plan. For drivers, compare the published paid lots and their time limits; once parked, the compact showroom route is designed to work on foot.

Compare the official schedule, tickets, and parking guidance, then use Perk Nation's design search or restaurant search to extend the day.

How to choose quickly

  • Final-day plan: choose Long Beach Greek Festival for food and culture, or Glendale's chess tournament if you are registered or have confirmed spectator access.
  • Fan and maker weekend: choose BlizzCon for the September 12-13 gaming commitment or Maker Faire Orange County for hands-on invention; for football, choose UCLA at the Rose Bowl.
  • Strategy and learning: choose Glendale's chess tournament, Maker Faire Orange County, or Arcadia's health fair.
  • Film and culture: choose Glendale's Armenian Film Festival for premieres and filmmaker conversations or Pasadena Greek Festival for food, music, and dance.
  • Free cultural evening: choose Arcadia's Mid-Autumn Moon Festival for performances, children's activities, food, and mooncake making.
  • Free art day: choose Pasadena ARTWalk for makers or the Chalk Festival for live mural-making.
  • Beach music: choose Ocean Way for a ticketed destination weekend, Americana for a free afternoon, or Tongva Twilight for a free evening.
  • Major museum opening: choose the Lucas Museum for narrative art, film, architecture, and a new Exposition Park garden.
  • Food event: choose Long Beach for a compact burger crawl, Orange County for the widest menu range, or Taste of Arcadia for one ticketed afternoon of local sampling.
  • Burbank: choose the Career Transitions Expo for industry value or the free Autumn Arts Festival for music, artists, and food trucks.
  • Live music: choose the Hollywood Bowl fireworks finale for production scale, Burbank's Autumn Arts Festival for a free neighborhood afternoon, or Glendale's Jewel City series for a free one-hour set.
  • Quieter culture: choose The Great Outdoors at Greystone.
  • Design and architecture: choose Design West Hollywood, especially the full September 30 program.
