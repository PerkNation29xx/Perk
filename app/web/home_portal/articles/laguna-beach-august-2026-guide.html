Laguna Beach guide · August 17-September 6

Laguna Beach has seven current late-summer arts plans worth the trip.

Four festival grounds, a one-of-a-kind stage production, a museum reset, and free promenade music turn Laguna Beach into Southern California's strongest choose-your-own arts day. This guide ranks the options by distinctiveness, value, and how easily each can anchor a full visit.

1. Pageant of the Masters: best once-only Laguna night

When: nightly at 8:30 p.m. through September 4. Best for: first-time visitors, theater fans, and anyone choosing one high-production evening.

The 2026 program, The Greatest of All Time, uses living performers, sets, lighting, narration, and a professional orchestra to recreate well-known works of art. It ranks first because the experience is specific to Laguna Beach and difficult to substitute with a gallery or concert elsewhere.

Practical review: reserve the performance before building dinner around it. The organizer says a Pageant ticket includes unlimited Festival of Arts access for the summer, so use the art show before the performance or return on another day if the ticket terms still allow it. The Passport to the Arts does not include the Pageant.

Check the official Pageant schedule and tickets, then browse Laguna Beach restaurants for a pre-show meal.

2. Sawdust Art Festival: best local-maker immersion

When: daily through September 6; hours run later on Fridays and Saturdays. Best for: families, shoppers who want work made by Laguna Beach artists, and visitors who value demonstrations as much as finished pieces.

Sawdust combines more than 180 local artists with live entertainment on three stages, artist demonstrations, hands-on workshops, food, and drinks. The eucalyptus-lined setting makes it the easiest festival to enjoy without treating every booth like a formal gallery stop.

Practical review: set a spending range before browsing and check the daily entertainment or class schedule if a specific activity matters. Go earlier for a slower look at the booths; choose Friday or Saturday evening for longer hours and a livelier atmosphere.

Review the current Sawdust dates, hours, admission, and program links.

3. Passport to the Arts: best way to compare three festivals

When: valid through September 6. Best for: first-time visitors who want to compare Festival of Arts, Sawdust, and Laguna Art-A-Fair instead of guessing which one fits.

The destination organization lists the Passport at $29, with one-time entry to all three participating festivals, a one-time Act V parking pass, and selected local discounts. It does not include Pageant of the Masters. That distinction matters: the Passport is a festival sampler, not an all-access Pageant package.

Practical review: the value is strongest if you will use all three admissions. Do not rush through three grounds in one short afternoon simply to complete the pass. Split the visits across a long day or more than one date if the current terms permit, and confirm where the pass is sold before arriving.

Use the official tourism Passport guide for current inclusions and restrictions.

4. Festival of Arts Fine Art Show: best concentrated Orange County survey

When: daily through September 3. Best for: collectors, serious browsers, and visitors who want a curated look at more than 100 Orange County artists.

The Fine Art Show is the strongest daytime pairing with a Pageant night because both share the same organization and grounds. Its focused regional roster makes comparisons easier than a broad marketplace: look across painting, photography, sculpture, jewelry, and other media before returning to a shortlist.

Practical review: check the live calendar for talks, demonstrations, workshops, or music that can improve the timing of your visit. A Pageant ticket carries specific Festival access benefits; general festival admission and reserved-event seating can follow different rules.

Use the official Festival of Arts calendar and visitor guidance.

5. Laguna Art-A-Fair: best broad artist mix

When: daily through September 6. Best for: visitors who want local, national, and international artists in one garden setting.

Laguna Art-A-Fair presents 125 artists and master craftspeople across painting, photography, sculpture, jewelry, glass, ceramics, and other media. It complements Sawdust rather than duplicating it: Sawdust is limited to Laguna Beach artists, while Art-A-Fair is intentionally wider.

Practical review: choose this festival when variety matters more than a strictly local roster. Admission is sold as a season ticket rather than a one-hour timed visit, and Pageant ticket holders receive same-day entry; confirm the current policy at purchase.

Check current Art-A-Fair dates, hours, prices, and access notes.

6. Laguna Art Museum: best focused indoor reset

When: current exhibitions run through late summer and beyond. Best for: a midday heat break, California-art context, and visitors who prefer a manageable museum to a full festival ground.

The current lineup includes de la Torre Brothers: The Tipping Point, Andy Moses's Into the Light, and Frank Cuprien's For the Love of the Sea. The mix creates a useful bridge between contemporary spectacle, light and color, and Laguna's long relationship with the coast.

Practical review: use the museum as a deliberate hour or two, not a filler squeezed between distant stops. Confirm normal hours, admission, and any dated program before planning around it.

Review the official exhibition list and museum event calendar.

7. Music at the Promenade: best flexible downtown add-on

When: Friday, Saturday, and Sunday evenings through August 30, generally 5:30-7:30 p.m. on Forest Avenue. Best for: visitors who want live music without committing the whole day to a scheduled performance.

The free downtown series works best as a connector between galleries, shopping, dinner, and the coast. Its drop-in format is exactly the advantage when a festival day runs long.

Practical review: verify the day's performer and any weather or schedule note, then treat the set as one part of the evening. Do not make a tight dinner reservation based on hearing every song; downtown movement and trolley timing deserve a buffer.

Check the City of Laguna Beach cultural-arts calendar.

Three ready-made Laguna Beach arts days

  • Signature first visit: Festival of Arts in late afternoon, an early dinner, then Pageant of the Masters at 8:30 p.m.
  • Maker-focused day: Sawdust before the evening rush, Laguna Art-A-Fair for a contrasting roster, then Music at the Promenade if energy allows.
  • Low-cost culture night: Laguna Art Museum, Music at the Promenade, and a trolley-supported downtown dinner without stacking ticketed festivals.

Parking and trolley strategy

Laguna Beach's summer routes run through September 7. The Canyon Route links Act V peripheral parking with the festivals and downtown, the Coastal Route serves Coast Highway, and the Summer Breeze connects a peripheral Irvine lot on selected days. Frequencies and service windows differ by route, so check the live page rather than assuming every trolley is equally frequent.

Practical review: choose one parking location and one festival cluster before leaving home. A car-light plan works best when the return route and final service time are settled first. Keep a screenshot or saved link to the live route page and allow extra time after the Pageant or a late festival close.

Before you go

  • Confirm admission, hours, and any reserved program.
  • Choose one festival cluster before deciding where to park.
  • Check the return trolley time before an evening performance.
  • Bring sun protection, water, and a coastal evening layer.
  • Do not assume the Passport includes the Pageant.
