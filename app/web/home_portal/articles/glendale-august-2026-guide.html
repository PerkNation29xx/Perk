Glendale has four remaining August plans that make the foothills feel close.

1. Brand Summer Concert Series: best free Friday music

When: Carl Verheyen Band closes the series Friday, August 28 at 7 p.m. behind Brand Library & Art Center. Best for: families, friends, and dates who want a picnic-and-music night without an admission charge.

Brand Library lists rock and jazz from Carl Verheyen Band for the finale. The concert runs 60-90 minutes without intermission, and visitors may bring chairs, blankets, and a picnic for the grassy hillside.

Practical review: this leads the ranking because it is current, free, and easy to pair with a local meal. Seats are not provided, parking is free but limited, ride-share is encouraged, and alcohol is prohibited. Arrive before 7 p.m. if lawn position matters.

2. Wander the Wilderness Bus: best car-light trail plan

When: Sunday, August 30 from Fremont Park. Service is listed hourly from 8:30 a.m.-1:30 p.m. Best for: households that want Deukmejian Wilderness Park access without managing a separate drive and parking plan.

The city lists the shuttle as free. Pacific Park departures start at 501 South Pacific Avenue; Fremont Park departures start at 600 Hahn Avenue. The service connects south Glendale with Deukmejian Wilderness Park on selected weekends.

Practical review: this is transportation, not a guided hike. Choose a trail that fits your party, check heat and air quality, carry more water than you expect to need, and leave enough time to catch the return service rather than relying on the final departure.

Use the official bus schedule and review the city's trail information.

3. Classic Film Under the Stars: best late-August lawn movie

When: Saturday, August 29 from 6-9 p.m. at Brand Park. Best for: classic-film fans, neighborhood groups, and anyone who prefers a picnic lawn to a multiplex.

The August selection is My Man Godfrey, presented on the lawn near the Doctors House by local film, history, and community organizations. The city lists the screening as free and encourages visitors to pack a picnic.

Practical review: the film and historic setting make this the strongest late-month cultural plan. Bring a low chair or blanket, a layer for after sunset, and food that does not require elaborate setup. Confirm any parking or weather update before going.

Review the official outdoor-film listing.

4. Montrose Harvest Market: best flexible Sunday start

When: Sunday, August 30 from 8 a.m.-1 p.m. in Montrose Shopping Park. Best for: a low-commitment morning that can flow into brunch, shopping, or a nearby trail plan.

The recurring market earns the final spot because it is easy to fit around other plans. Vendor mix and availability can vary from week to week, which is part of the browsing experience but not a reason to promise a particular product.

Practical review: go earlier for cooler temperatures and better selection. Bring a reusable bag, keep the rest of the morning flexible, and pair the market with one nearby meal rather than turning it into a cross-county itinerary.

Confirm the date on the official August city calendar, then compare Glendale dining options.

Three ready-made Glendale days

  • Friday concert picnic: choose Carl Verheyen Band on August 28, arrive before 7 p.m., and keep the lawn setup compact.
  • Market Sunday: shop the August 30 Montrose Harvest Market early, then keep brunch and shopping in the same neighborhood.
  • Picnic evening: choose the August 29 classic film, bring a compact lawn setup, and add an early Glendale meal.
  • Sunday market and lunch: shop the Montrose market early, then choose one restaurant through the Glendale directory.

Before you go

  • RSVP where the city requests it; free does not always mean walk-up.
  • Recheck heat, air quality, trail access, and weather before foothill plans.
  • Bring water, sun protection, and layers suited to the time of day.
  • Confirm the final venue and time for concerts and community programs.
  • Choose one geographic cluster instead of stacking distant plans.
