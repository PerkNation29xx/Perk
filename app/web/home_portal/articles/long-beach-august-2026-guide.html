Long Beach guide · July 31-August 22

Long Beach has more than a month of summer plans. These nine are worth building a day around.

Food Scene Week starts July 31 with more than 100 restaurants, cafes, and pop-ups. August then moves through waterfront jazz, street tastings, outdoor films, neighborhood concerts, and a downtown food-and-DJ festival. This guide ranks the strongest plans by range, value, and how easily each one can become a complete Long Beach day.

1. Long Beach Food Scene Week: best way to sample the city's range

When: Friday, July 31 through Sunday, August 9. Best for: diners who want a choose-your-own itinerary rather than a single crowded festival day.

Visit Long Beach says more than 100 restaurants, cafes, and pop-ups are participating with special dishes, prix-fixe menus, discounts, and collaborations. The organizer is clear that this is not only a discount week: many kitchens use it to test dishes, revive a favorite, or show a part of their cooking that is not normally on the menu.

The most useful approach is to pick a theme and limit the route. The official tourism guide calls out the Cambodian Cowboy Dry Noodles collaboration from Phnom Penh Noodle Shack and Battambong BBQ, prix-fixe programs at Sky Room and Ammatoli, and first-time participants including Appu's Turmeric Cafe, Colossus, Viaje, Steelhead Coffee, and Joe Jost's. Those are source-backed starting points, not star-rated endorsements. Compare current menus, price, service hours, dietary information, and reservation rules before choosing.

Practical review: rank one hard-to-repeat special first, then add a nearby dessert, coffee, or casual stop. Three distant reservations can turn a food day into a driving day. Use the official participant explorer, read Visit Long Beach's current highlights, and compare Long Beach restaurant listings.

2. Long Beach Jazz Festival: best ticketed waterfront weekend

When: August 8-9 at the Long Beach Amphitheater, 1051 Queens Highway. Best for: music fans who want one clear anchor and can build meals around a fixed performance day.

Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the festival's 36th year and says the weekend combines acclaimed jazz performers with food, art, and a Health and Wellness Pavilion. That range makes it the strongest large-format music plan here, but it requires more commitment than a neighborhood concert: confirm the daily lineup, entry time, seating format, bag rules, re-entry policy, and weather guidance before purchasing.

Practical review: treat the show as the center of the day and schedule only one meal beforehand. Food Scene Week overlaps the full weekend, so the cleanest pairing is a restaurant close to the waterfront or your parking route—not a cross-city reservation with a tight clock.

Check the current Visit Long Beach festival overview and browse nearby dining options.

3. Stroll & Savor: best casual tasting crawl

When: August 19-20 on Second Street in Belmont Shore. Best for: friends, dates, and families who want small bites without committing to one full menu.

Visit Long Beach says dozens of eateries set up sidewalk tasting stations and that a sheet of 12 tickets costs $15 online or at the Chase Bank at 5200 Second Street during the event. The format is simple, but tickets are finite: scan the current stops, choose a few priorities, and reserve enough time to walk rather than trying to sample every block.

Practical review: this ranks above the downtown tasting event for flexibility because it runs on two evenings. It is best when the group agrees on two or three must-tries and treats everything else as a discovery. Confirm the latest ticket and event details before arriving.

Use Visit Long Beach's summer series and the Long Beach directory for a full meal or shop nearby.

4. Taste of Downtown with KCRW Summer Nights: best food-and-DJ street festival

When: Saturday, August 22, 2-10 p.m. on Pine Avenue between Broadway and Third Street. Best for: groups that value atmosphere, music, and casual samples as much as a seated meal.

Visit Long Beach lists the evening as free and all ages, with Taste of Downtown leading into KCRW DJ sets by Nassir Nassirzadeh and Raul Campos. Food and drinks are sold separately. The format works well for mixed-interest groups because people can sample, listen, and move at their own pace.

Practical review: arrive earlier for easier food browsing, then stay for music. Do not promise the group a particular restaurant unless it appears on the current vendor list, and confirm ticket requirements for tastings even though general event admission is free.

Read the official tourism event listing and compare downtown dining options.

5. Moonlight Movies on the Beach: best low-cost evening

When: A League of Their Own on August 4 and a Grease sing-along on August 18 at Granada Beach. Screenings begin at sunset, between 8 and 8:30 p.m. Best for: dates, families with older children, and groups that can bring their own picnic setup.

The official tourism listing says admission is free, outside food is welcome, local food trucks and kettle corn are available, and Granada Beach parking becomes free after 6 p.m. Limited parking is the real constraint. Bring layers, a blanket or low chair, and enough time to settle before the screen starts.

Practical review: this is the best value plan in the guide, but not the most predictable. Wind, sand, darkness, and parking are part of the experience. Recheck the organizer for weather changes before leaving.

Confirm the current movie schedule.

6. City-run summer programs: best family and neighborhood choice

When: programs continue through August 21 at parks across Long Beach. Best for: families seeking free or low-cost activities close to home.

The City of Long Beach lists free Movies in the Park, free youth recreation swim, free weekday Summer Fun Days and Be S.A.F.E. activities, $5 El Dorado Nature Center concerts on August 6 and 20, and a free Dance Fest at Cesar E. Chavez Park on August 15. Each program has its own location, age guidance, registration, and arrival rules.

Practical review: choose by neighborhood and age first, then by headline. A nearby park movie with a short trip can beat a larger event that requires parking and a late return. For supervised or aquatic programs, read the city's participation and adult-accompaniment rules in full.

Use the City of Long Beach summer-program announcement.

7. Jazz on the Bay: best free music-on-the-sand plan

When: Saturday, August 22 at Harbor Side Beach, 98 66th Place. Best for: jazz listeners who prefer a small waterfront setting to a ticketed festival.

Visit Long Beach lists vocalist Niki Haris for the August date in a free series staged directly on the sand along Alamitos Bay. The setting is the draw: music, sailboats, and sunset in one place. It is also less controlled than a seated venue, so bring a beach-appropriate setup and check the organizer's current start time and access notes.

Practical review: choose this over Taste of Downtown if the performance itself is the priority; choose Taste of Downtown if food variety and a street-festival crowd matter more. Doing both would require a rushed cross-city plan.

Review the current Jazz on the Bay listing.

8. Jaws on the Queen Mary: best landmark movie setting

When: Thursday, August 13. Best for: movie fans who care as much about the shipboard setting as the film.

Visit Long Beach lists Jaws as the August Movies on Deck selection aboard the Queen Mary. This is a more distinctive setting than a standard outdoor movie, but it can also involve ship admission, timed access, parking, and event-specific ticket rules.

Practical review: confirm what the movie ticket includes and how early the deck opens before planning dinner. Pair it with a waterfront meal only when the schedule leaves a generous buffer.

Start with Visit Long Beach's current summer-series page and then follow its Queen Mary calendar link for final access details.

9. Little Earth Cinema at the Art Theatre: best daytime kids' movie

When: Wednesdays at 11 a.m. through August 19 at the Art Theatre on Retro Row. Best for: children, teens, animation fans, and adults who want a daytime indoor option.

Visit Long Beach says the environmental-animation series is free for children and teens and $5 for adults. The published summer lineup includes Studio Ghibli films and other family animation, but titles vary by date. Check the Art Theatre's current calendar before promising a particular movie.

Practical review: this is the easiest heat-proof choice in the ranking. Add lunch or a short Retro Row walk instead of stacking another major event afterward.

Confirm details through Visit Long Beach's summer guide.

Three ready-made Long Beach days

  • Food-first weekend: one Food Scene Week special, a coffee or dessert stop nearby, then a waterfront walk with no second reservation.
  • Low-cost evening: an early casual meal, Granada Beach before parking tightens, then Moonlight Movies with layers and a picnic blanket.
  • August 22 choice: Jazz on the Bay for focused live music, or Taste of Downtown for food samples and DJs—pick one anchor and leave room around it.

Before you go

  • Confirm menus and restaurant hours on Food Scene Week's live explorer.
  • Check tickets, age rules, bags, and re-entry for ticketed festivals.
  • Recheck weather and parking for beach and park programs.
  • Choose one geographic cluster instead of crossing the city repeatedly.
  • Treat prices on organizer pages as current only after rechecking.
