Long Beach guide · August 21-30

Long Beach has seven late-August plans worth building a day around.

August 21 starts with live music in the Japanese Garden, followed by Saturday beach jazz, a downtown food-and-DJ festival, family park programs, major waterfront concerts, and a two-day blues finale. This guide ranks the strongest current plans by range, value, and how easily each one can become a complete Long Beach day.

1. Taste of Downtown with KCRW Summer Nights: best food-and-DJ street festival

When: Saturday, August 22, 2-10 p.m. on Pine Avenue between Broadway and Third Street. Best for: groups that value atmosphere, music, and casual samples as much as a seated meal.

Visit Long Beach lists the evening as free and all ages, with Taste of Downtown leading into KCRW DJ sets by Nassir Nassirzadeh and Raul Campos. Food and drinks are sold separately. The format works well for mixed-interest groups because people can sample, listen, and move at their own pace.

Practical review: arrive earlier for easier food browsing, then stay for music. Do not promise the group a particular restaurant unless it appears on the current vendor list, and confirm ticket requirements for tastings even though general event admission is free.

Read the official tourism event listing and compare downtown dining options.

2. Jazz on the Bay: best free music-on-the-sand plan

When: Saturday, August 22 at Harbor Side Beach, 98 66th Place. Best for: jazz listeners who prefer a small waterfront setting to a ticketed festival.

Visit Long Beach lists vocalist Niki Haris for the August date in a free series staged directly on the sand along Alamitos Bay. The setting is the draw: music, sailboats, and sunset in one place. It is also less controlled than a seated venue, so bring a beach-appropriate setup and check the organizer's current start time and access notes.

Practical review: choose this over Taste of Downtown if the performance itself is the priority; choose Taste of Downtown if food variety and a street-festival crowd matter more. Doing both would require a rushed cross-city plan.

Review the current Jazz on the Bay listing.

3. Japanese Garden summer concert: best slower Friday picnic

When: Friday, August 21 from 5:30-7:30 p.m. at the Earl Burns Miller Japanese Garden. Best for: dates, small groups, and listeners who want a calm garden setting rather than a large street festival.

Visit Long Beach lists Think of One Jazz for the August 21 date in the garden's summer concert-and-picnic series. Admission is listed at $10, with advance reservations available and a limited number of chairs at the venue.

Practical review: reserve before traveling, bring a compact picnic setup, and arrive early enough to enjoy the garden. The short two-hour window makes this a strong same-day choice without taking over the entire night.

Confirm the current Japanese Garden concert schedule, then compare Long Beach restaurants.

4. Three Pianos: best career-spanning indie-rock night

When: Saturday, August 22 at 8 p.m. at Long Beach Amphitheater. Best for: fans of Something Corporate, Jack's Mannequin, and Andrew McMahon in the Wilderness who want all three eras in one concert.

Visit Long Beach describes a career-spanning show built around Andrew McMahon's three projects and notes that one dollar from every ticket supports the Dear Jack Foundation. It is the clearest ticketed alternative to the free Saturday food and jazz events.

Practical review: choose this only if the music is the priority. Check current ticket inventory, venue entry rules, transportation, and the final run time before pairing it with another Saturday event.

Use the official tourism event listing.

5. City-run summer programs: best family and neighborhood choice

When: remaining Friday programs continue August 21 at parks across Long Beach. Best for: families seeking free or low-cost activities close to home.

The City of Long Beach lists an August 21 Movies in the Park date, free youth recreation swim, and free weekday Summer Fun Days and Be S.A.F.E. activities. Each program has its own location, age guidance, registration, and arrival rules.

Practical review: choose by neighborhood and age first, then by headline. A nearby park movie with a short trip can beat a larger event that requires parking and a late return. For supervised or aquatic programs, read the city's participation and adult-accompaniment rules in full.

Use the City of Long Beach summer-program announcement.

6. New Blues Festival: best two-day waterfront music finale

When: Saturday and Sunday, August 29-30 at Shoreline Aquatic Park. Best for: blues listeners who want a full festival day with waterfront views, food trucks, and a vendor village.

Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the festival's tenth anniversary and lists both local and international performers. The setting across from the Queen Mary gives the weekend a strong sense of place, but a two-day music festival still requires more planning than the free neighborhood concerts in this guide.

Practical review: compare the daily lineups before choosing a one-day or weekend ticket, then check the organizer's current entry time, seating format, shade rules, bag policy, re-entry, and accessibility information. Build the rest of the day close to the waterfront rather than adding a cross-city reservation.

Review Visit Long Beach's current music overview and browse Long Beach dining options.

7. Nas & The Roots with De La Soul: best major hip-hop night

When: Friday, August 28 at Long Beach Amphitheater. Best for: hip-hop fans who want a destination concert rather than a casual neighborhood program.

Visit Long Beach lists Nas and The Roots with De La Soul for the waterfront amphitheater's first season. The lineup is the reason to choose this plan, while ticket cost, venue access, and the late return make it a higher-commitment option.

Practical review: use the venue's current ticket path, review the bag and entry rules, and plan transportation before buying. Keep dinner close to the waterfront so the evening does not depend on a cross-city connection.

Check Visit Long Beach's amphitheater guide.

Three ready-made Long Beach days

  • Friday garden evening: reserve the Japanese Garden concert, bring a compact picnic, and use the Long Beach directory for a flexible meal afterward.
  • August 22 free choice: Jazz on the Bay for focused live music, or Taste of Downtown for food samples and DJs—pick one anchor and leave room around it.
  • Ticketed music night: choose Three Pianos for indie-rock nostalgia, Nas and The Roots for hip-hop, or the New Blues Festival for a full waterfront weekend.
