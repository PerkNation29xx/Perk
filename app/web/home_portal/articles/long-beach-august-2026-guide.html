Long Beach has four late-August plans worth building a day around.

Reviewed by Perk Nation Editorial · Updated August 26, 2026 · How we rank and verify recommendations

1. Nas & The Roots with De La Soul: best major hip-hop night

When: Friday, August 28 at Long Beach Amphitheater. Best for: hip-hop fans who want a destination concert rather than a casual neighborhood program.

Visit Long Beach lists Nas and The Roots with De La Soul for the waterfront amphitheater's first season. The lineup is the reason to choose this plan, while ticket cost, venue access, and the late return make it a higher-commitment option.

Practical review: use the venue's current ticket path, review the bag and entry rules, and plan transportation before buying. Keep dinner close to the waterfront so the evening does not depend on a cross-city connection.

Check Visit Long Beach's amphitheater guide.

2. New Blues Festival: best two-day waterfront music finale

When: Saturday and Sunday, August 29-30 at Shoreline Aquatic Park. Best for: blues listeners who want a full festival day with waterfront views, food trucks, and a vendor village.

Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the festival's tenth anniversary and lists both local and international performers. The setting across from the Queen Mary gives the weekend a strong sense of place, but a two-day music festival still requires more planning than the free neighborhood concerts in this guide.

Practical review: compare the daily lineups before choosing a one-day or weekend ticket, then check the organizer's current entry time, seating format, shade rules, bag policy, re-entry, and accessibility information. Build the rest of the day close to the waterfront rather than adding a cross-city reservation.

Review Visit Long Beach's current music overview and browse Long Beach dining options.

3. Long Beach Film Festival: best new citywide film weekend

When: Saturday and Sunday, August 29-30 at venues around Long Beach. Best for: film fans who want a mix of fiction, documentary, feature-length, and short-form work.

Visit Long Beach describes the inaugural festival as a citywide gathering of filmmakers, audiences, and local businesses. That breadth is the appeal, but it also means the live program and venue map should determine the day.

Practical review: choose screenings before making meal reservations, group events by neighborhood, and confirm whether each program needs a separate ticket. A citywide festival works best with one or two anchors, not an attempt to cross town for every session.

Use Visit Long Beach's current August event guide for the official festival path.

4. Conscience: best history-minded theater

When: August 26-September 13 at International City Theatre. Best for: theatergoers interested in political history, principled dissent, and a focused indoor evening.

Visit Long Beach describes Joe DiPietro's play about Senator Margaret Chase Smith and her 1950 “Declaration of Conscience” speech challenging Joseph McCarthy's Red Scare tactics. It adds a thoughtful indoor option to a week otherwise dominated by outdoor music.

Practical review: verify the selected performance time and ticket inventory, then leave room after the show rather than stacking another event. Review content notes and accessibility details directly with the theater if anyone in the group needs them.

Start with Visit Long Beach's current listing and follow its venue link for tickets.

Three ready-made Long Beach days

  • Opening-week theater: choose a Conscience performance, confirm the showtime and ticket tier, and keep dinner near the convention center.
  • Waterfront music night: choose Nas and The Roots for hip-hop or the New Blues Festival for a full festival day; do not treat them as interchangeable commitments.
  • Film or theater: pick one Long Beach Film Festival screening cluster or one Conscience performance, then build the meal around that venue.
