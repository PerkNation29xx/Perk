Long Beach guide · August 19-30

Long Beach has seven late-August plans worth building a day around.

The second half of August moves from sidewalk tastings and outdoor films to free beach jazz, park programs, a downtown food-and-DJ festival, and a two-day blues finale. This guide ranks the strongest current plans by range, value, and how easily each one can become a complete Long Beach day.

1. Stroll & Savor: best casual tasting crawl

When: August 19-20 on Second Street in Belmont Shore. Best for: friends, dates, and families who want small bites without committing to one full menu.

Visit Long Beach says dozens of eateries set up sidewalk tasting stations and that a sheet of 12 tickets costs $15 online or at the Chase Bank at 5200 Second Street during the event. The format is simple, but tickets are finite: scan the current stops, choose a few priorities, and reserve enough time to walk rather than trying to sample every block.

Practical review: this ranks above the downtown tasting event for flexibility because it runs on two evenings. It is best when the group agrees on two or three must-tries and treats everything else as a discovery. Confirm the latest ticket and event details before arriving.

Use Visit Long Beach's summer series and the Long Beach directory for a full meal or shop nearby.

2. Taste of Downtown with KCRW Summer Nights: best food-and-DJ street festival

When: Saturday, August 22, 2-10 p.m. on Pine Avenue between Broadway and Third Street. Best for: groups that value atmosphere, music, and casual samples as much as a seated meal.

Visit Long Beach lists the evening as free and all ages, with Taste of Downtown leading into KCRW DJ sets by Nassir Nassirzadeh and Raul Campos. Food and drinks are sold separately. The format works well for mixed-interest groups because people can sample, listen, and move at their own pace.

Practical review: arrive earlier for easier food browsing, then stay for music. Do not promise the group a particular restaurant unless it appears on the current vendor list, and confirm ticket requirements for tastings even though general event admission is free.

Read the official tourism event listing and compare downtown dining options.

3. New Blues Festival: best two-day waterfront music finale

When: Saturday and Sunday, August 29-30 at Shoreline Aquatic Park. Best for: blues listeners who want a full festival day with waterfront views, food trucks, and a vendor village.

Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the festival's tenth anniversary and lists both local and international performers. The setting across from the Queen Mary gives the weekend a strong sense of place, but a two-day music festival still requires more planning than the free neighborhood concerts in this guide.

Practical review: compare the daily lineups before choosing a one-day or weekend ticket, then check the organizer's current entry time, seating format, shade rules, bag policy, re-entry, and accessibility information. Build the rest of the day close to the waterfront rather than adding a cross-city reservation.

Review Visit Long Beach's current music overview and browse Long Beach dining options.

4. Beachside Beats Fest: best free family-friendly concert

When: Thursday, August 20 at Seaport Way at 2ND & PCH. Seating opens at 4 p.m.; free face painting and airbrush tattoos run from 5-7 p.m. Best for: families, Prince fans, and groups that want a free concert with an easy food-and-shopping backup plan.

Visit Long Beach lists Purple Generation, a Prince tribute band, for this installment of the summer series. Chairs and blankets are welcome, and the first-come seating format rewards an earlier arrival without turning the evening into an all-day commitment.

Practical review: this is the strongest no-admission music choice before the weekend. Arrive near seating time if a clear view matters, but keep the plan flexible: use the surrounding center for food and a break rather than claiming lawn space hours early.

Confirm the current Beachside Beats Fest listing.

5. City-run summer programs: best family and neighborhood choice

When: programs continue from August 19 through August 21 at parks across Long Beach. Best for: families seeking free or low-cost activities close to home.

The City of Long Beach lists free Movies in the Park on August 19 and 21; free youth recreation swim; free weekday Summer Fun Days and Be S.A.F.E. activities; and a $5 El Dorado Nature Center concert on August 20. Each program has its own location, age guidance, registration, and arrival rules.

Practical review: choose by neighborhood and age first, then by headline. A nearby park movie with a short trip can beat a larger event that requires parking and a late return. For supervised or aquatic programs, read the city's participation and adult-accompaniment rules in full.

Use the City of Long Beach summer-program announcement.

6. Jazz on the Bay: best free music-on-the-sand plan

When: Saturday, August 22 at Harbor Side Beach, 98 66th Place. Best for: jazz listeners who prefer a small waterfront setting to a ticketed festival.

Visit Long Beach lists vocalist Niki Haris for the August date in a free series staged directly on the sand along Alamitos Bay. The setting is the draw: music, sailboats, and sunset in one place. It is also less controlled than a seated venue, so bring a beach-appropriate setup and check the organizer's current start time and access notes.

Practical review: choose this over Taste of Downtown if the performance itself is the priority; choose Taste of Downtown if food variety and a street-festival crowd matter more. Doing both would require a rushed cross-city plan.

Review the current Jazz on the Bay listing.

7. Little Earth Cinema at the Art Theatre: best daytime kids' movie

When: Wednesdays at 11 a.m. through August 19 at the Art Theatre on Retro Row. Best for: children, teens, animation fans, and adults who want a daytime indoor option.

Visit Long Beach says the environmental-animation series is free for children and teens and $5 for adults. The published summer lineup includes Studio Ghibli films and other family animation, but titles vary by date. Check the Art Theatre's current calendar before promising a particular movie.

Practical review: this is the easiest heat-proof choice in the ranking. Add lunch or a short Retro Row walk instead of stacking another major event afterward.

Confirm details through Visit Long Beach's summer guide.

Three ready-made Long Beach days

  • Food-first evenings: use Stroll & Savor for a flexible Belmont Shore tasting walk, or Taste of Downtown for samples plus KCRW DJs.
  • Low-cost evening: choose Beachside Beats for a free concert on August 20, or use a nearby city park movie when a shorter neighborhood trip matters more.
  • August 22 choice: Jazz on the Bay for focused live music, or Taste of Downtown for food samples and DJs—pick one anchor and leave room around it.
