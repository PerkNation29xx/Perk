Los Angeles cultural guide · August 15-23

Nisei Week brings two weekends of Japanese American culture to Little Tokyo. Start with the events that fit your day.

The 84th annual festival combines the Grand Parade, free family programming, traditional arts, live entertainment, dance, food, and community gathering. The useful choice is not whether to see everything; it is whether your group wants an activity-led family day, parade afternoon, arts visit, plaza festival, or the Ondo closing celebration.

1. Grand Parade: best signature Nisei Week experience

When: Sunday, August 16, 4-6 p.m. Best for: first-time festival visitors and groups who want the largest public expression of Nisei Week.

The official route starts on Central Avenue, travels west on Second Street, turns north on San Pedro Street, and then heads east on First Street. That loop makes the parade easy to pair with an earlier JACCC arts visit or Little Tokyo meal without moving the car.

Practical review: the pageantry and community turnout make this the top choice, while afternoon heat and a busy curb are the tradeoffs. Arrive early, choose one viewing area instead of chasing the procession, bring water, and expect street access to change around the route.

2. JANM's Natsumatsuri Family Festival: best family day

When: Saturday, August 15, 11 a.m.-3 p.m. at Terasaki Budokan. Best for: families, first-time visitors, and groups that want several hands-on activities in one free program.

This ranks first because the schedule makes the day easy to understand. JANM lists a community bon odori dance from 11:30 a.m.-12:30 p.m., Cold Tofu improv at 1 p.m., Kotolan at 1:45 p.m., and J-Town Taiko Club at 2:30 p.m. Crafts, a photo booth, a Paul Frank stamp rally, and other activities fill the gaps.

The event is free, but JANM offers a ticket link and notes that some activities are available while supplies last. Reserve through the organizer, arrive near the opening if crafts matter, and do not rely on the museum pavilion for a normal visit: JANM says the pavilion remains closed for renovation while programs continue elsewhere.

Review the official Natsumatsuri schedule and free-ticket link, then find food and services through Los Angeles restaurant listings.

3. JACCC's August 15-16 arts weekend: best traditional-arts sampler

When: Saturday and Sunday, August 15-16, 11 a.m.-4 p.m. Best for: visitors who want a quieter cultural plan with several traditional art forms in one building.

JACCC lists ikebana, tea ceremony, a sword exhibition, Japanese dolls, and Japanese ceramics on both days. Admission is free and no reservation is required. Tea-ceremony viewing is free; matcha and sweets are listed at $10, with cash preferred.

This is the best companion to Natsumatsuri on August 15, but it also works as a lower-pressure Sunday plan. Pick two or three rooms rather than rushing every display, then leave time for lunch or shopping nearby.

Check JACCC's complete two-weekend schedule. Perk Nation currently has 61 Los Angeles listings for the rest of the day.

4. Plaza Festival: best all-day Saturday gathering

When: Saturday, August 22, 11 a.m.-8 p.m. at the JACCC plaza. Best for: live entertainment, community energy, and visitors who want a single anchor that lasts into the evening.

Nisei Week lists live entertainment throughout the day. JACCC's same weekend also includes bonsai, calligraphy, kintsugi, and Saturday sashiko programming, so visitors can alternate between the plaza and focused arts demonstrations.

Practical review: this is the strongest morning-to-evening option, but there is no reason to stand in one place for nine hours. Start at the market, choose the entertainment and demonstrations that matter, take a meal break, and check each host's current schedule.

Confirm the official Plaza Festival hours and review JACCC's arts schedule.

5. Ondo and Closing Ceremony: best festival finale

When: Sunday, August 23, 4-8 p.m. Best for: community dance, a lively closing celebration, and visitors who want a participatory finale.

The official event page places the celebration on First Street and notes a street closure beginning at 11:30 a.m. between San Pedro Street and Central Avenue. Build extra walking time into any museum, meal, or JACCC plan earlier that day.

Practical review: the ondo is more participatory than a seated show. Wear comfortable shoes, follow the dancers' lead, respect the working neighborhood, and use transit if a First Street closure would complicate parking.

Check the official Ondo and Closing Ceremony details.

6. Nisei Week Coronation: best opening-night pageantry

When: Saturday, August 15, 6:30-9 p.m. at the Tom Bradley Theatre in the Los Angeles Theatre Center, 514 South Spring Street. Best for: visitors who value formal community tradition and an evening program.

The Coronation and Awards Ceremony opens the festival's headline weekend. It is a more structured and formal choice than the free daytime programs, so confirm current ticket access and arrive with enough time for downtown parking or transit.

Practical review: choose this for the ceremony itself, not as a casual drop-in. Pair it with Natsumatsuri or JACCC earlier only if your group has the stamina for a full day and a venue change.

Review the official Coronation time, venue, and access details.

7. Little Tokyo Farmers' Market: best Saturday morning start

When: Saturdays August 1, 8, 15, and 22, 9 a.m.-2 p.m. at Isamu Noguchi Plaza. Best for: visitors who want fresh produce and a community stop before afternoon programming.

JACCC and Food Access LA describe a market centered on locally grown produce, Japanese American farmers, nutrition and wellness resources, and community connection. SNAP and EBT are accepted. On August 15 or 22, the market can turn a festival visit into a full day without adding another distant neighborhood.

The practical review: arrive earlier than the JACCC programs, shop before the plaza gets busier, and carry only what you are comfortable taking through an afternoon of exhibitions and performances.

See the official market schedule and access notes.

8. Rilakkuma at Okayama Kobo: best character-fan add-on

When: Sunday, August 23, with appearances and limited meet-and-greet reservations announced by Nisei Week. Best for: character fans who are already planning the closing Sunday.

This ranks as an add-on because reservation capacity is limited and the organizer's current announcement should determine availability. Do not make it the only reason for a long trip; pair it with JACCC arts or the Ondo finale and keep a no-reservation backup.

Practical review: check the latest Nisei Week announcement before leaving, allow extra time around First Street closures, and treat any merchandise or appearance access as subject to capacity.

Three ready-made plans

  • Family Saturday, August 15: reserve the free Natsumatsuri ticket, choose two performance times, then add one JACCC exhibition.
  • Signature Sunday, August 16: use JACCC as the morning anchor, eat before the rush, then choose one Grand Parade viewing area.
  • Festival finale, August 23: see JACCC arts, check Rilakkuma reservation availability, then join the Ondo closing celebration.

Transit, timing, and a respectful visit

JACCC is about a seven-minute walk from the Little Tokyo/Arts District station on Metro's A and E lines. Parking can fill during large neighborhood events, so compare transit with the Nisei Week map before choosing. Bring water, dress for outdoor walking, and check accessibility contacts on the organizer pages if anyone in your group needs accommodations.

Nisei Week is a living community celebration, not a themed backdrop. Give performances and demonstrations your attention, ask before photographing artists or participants at close range, and support neighborhood businesses when you stop for food or shopping.
