August 2026 event guide

Fourteen Southern California summer plans worth putting on the calendar.

Late August stretches from Laguna Beach art festivals and adaptive surfing to open-water swimming, waterfront food, garden concerts, outdoor movies, local markets, and Japanese American cultural programming. Orange County now has a complete seven-plan county guide; Huntington Beach, Laguna Beach, Santa Monica, Arcadia, Glendale, Pasadena, Long Beach, South Pasadena, and Little Tokyo also have dedicated planning guides. Every pick is ranked for timing, range, and how easily it can become a complete local day out.

1. South Pasadena crawl, market, and performance nights: best compact local loop

When: the Eat! Shop! Enjoy! crawl begins August 22-23, the certified farmers market runs August 20 and 27, and weekly singer-songwriter and musical-theater rooms continue Thursday and Friday. Best for: a food-and-retail discovery weekend, a market-and-dinner evening, or a short local plan with minimal driving between stops.

The citywide crawl connects restaurants, retailers, and live music, while the Thursday market, weekly open mics, and August 21 LEGO Free Play offer smaller ways into the city. South Pasadena's 97 live directory listings give the plan enough local context to stay useful beyond one event.

Use the complete five-plan South Pasadena guide, confirm dates on the South Pasadena Chamber calendar, and browse 97 South Pasadena listings.

2. Long Beach's food, film, and music run: best late-August variety

When: tonight's August 18 Grease sing-along leads into Stroll & Savor August 19-20, Taste of Downtown and Jazz on the Bay August 22, and the New Blues Festival August 29-30. Best for: people choosing between a free beach film, casual tastings, a downtown food-and-DJ festival, or destination music.

Moonlight Movies closes its season tonight at Granada Beach, while Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the New Blues Festival's tenth anniversary. Treat each as a separate plan: confirm weather and parking for the film, tasting-ticket details for Stroll & Savor, and entry, seating, shade, bag, re-entry, and accessibility rules for the music festival.

Use the complete seven-plan Long Beach August guide to compare the festival with Stroll & Savor, Taste of Downtown, Jazz on the Bay, movies, and family programs. Then review Visit Long Beach's current music overview and browse 208 Long Beach directory listings.

3. Santa Monica swims, movies, and Pier mornings: best coastal reset

When: Sunset Swim returns August 21; Wellness & Waves and Cinema by the Sea run on Saturdays through late August. Best for: a coastal day that can lean toward movement, swimming, local history, a market, or an outdoor movie.

The recurring programs make the rest of the month flexible. Wellness & Waves is listed as free and all ages from 8-10 a.m. at the Pier, while the remaining adult Sunset Swim and the hotel-led movie series require separate planning.

Use the complete six-plan Santa Monica late-summer guide for transit, weather, film, and reservation notes. Then check Wellness & Waves and the official Beach House schedule.

4. Glendale trails, campfire, market, and outdoor film: best low-key local calendar

When: trail and park programs run August 22-30, and Classic Film Under the Stars returns August 29. Best for: residents who want a community-scale outdoor plan without a full festival day.

The remaining calendar ranges from the wilderness shuttle and family campfire to the Sunday market and Brand Park lawn movie. Choose one foothill, market, or lawn plan and recheck heat, air quality, and the live city listing before leaving.

Use the complete four-plan Glendale August guide, open Glendale's official shuttle schedule, and explore 100 Glendale listings.

5. Friday Nights at The Gamble House: best architecture date night

When: second and fourth Fridays through August 28. Best for: architecture fans, quieter date nights, and Pasadena visitors who want a focused cultural stop before dinner.

The appeal is quality over scale: an evening at a landmark property can anchor a Pasadena plan without taking the entire day. Space and access may be limited, so confirm the available date before making dining reservations.

Check Visit Pasadena's event page, then choose among 563 Pasadena directory listings or open the Pasadena dining guide.

6. Concerts on Cañon in Beverly Hills: best free early-evening music

When: Thursdays through August 20, 6-8 p.m. Best for: a no-ticket concert paired with dinner, dessert, or a Golden Triangle walk.

The August 20 finale features The Tokens and is one of the easiest no-ticket plans in the guide. Arrive early if seating matters, and pair the two-hour concert with one nearby meal rather than overbuilding the evening.

Confirm Concerts on Cañon with the city, then browse Beverly Hills or compare the Beverly Hills dining guide.

7. Pasadena POPS at the Arboretum: best picnic concert night

When: August 29; picnicking begins at 5:30 p.m. and the concert begins at 7:30 p.m. Best for: music fans who want to make dinner part of the show rather than rush through it beforehand.

Music of the Night closes the Pasadena POPS season with Andrew Lloyd Webber and friends. The LA County Arboretum setting is the real planning advantage: arrive when gates open, bring or arrange a picnic within the venue rules, and treat the concert as the center of a slower night. Tickets are required, so check availability before making food plans.

Review the 2026 Pasadena POPS schedule, then compare 563 Pasadena listings, 40 Arcadia listings, or the Pasadena dining guide.

8. Stroll & Savor in Belmont Shore: best casual tasting crawl

When: August 19-20. Best for: groups that would rather sample several 2nd Street businesses than commit to one long dinner.

Visit Long Beach says dozens of eateries set up sidewalk tasting stations, with 12-ticket sheets sold online or at the event. That makes it approachable, but not unlimited: scan the participating stops first, choose a few must-tries, and leave time to walk Belmont Shore between bites.

Plan with Visit Long Beach's summer series, then browse 208 Long Beach directory listings for a full meal, shopping, or services nearby.

9. LA Jazz Festival's Jazz on the Beach: best destination music weekend

When: August 22-23, 10 a.m.-9 p.m. Best for: listeners who want a full-day lineup and a beach setting rather than a short evening set.

Discover Los Angeles lists John Legend, Janelle Monáe, Raphael Saadiq, P-Funk, and more at Dockweiler Beach. This is the higher-commitment music pick: admission is ticketed, the listed price range varies widely, and a long beach day needs advance transit, sun, seating, and return-trip planning.

Check the official tourism listing and organizer link, then explore 61 Los Angeles listings or the Playa del Rey dining guide.

10. Taste of Downtown Long Beach: best food-and-DJ street festival

When: August 22, 2-10 p.m. Best for: people who want local restaurant samples first and KCRW DJ sets later.

The Pine Avenue event combines bite-size food samples, beer, wine, cocktails, and evening music. It ranks below Stroll & Savor for flexibility because it is a single Saturday, but it is the stronger choice if live DJs and a downtown street-festival feel matter as much as the food.

Confirm Taste of Downtown with Visit Long Beach, then use the Long Beach directory or Long Beach dining guide to plan what comes before or after.

11. Just Like Heaven in Pasadena: best one-day indie festival

When: August 22 at Brookside at the Rose Bowl. Best for: fans who want The Strokes, LCD Soundsystem, TV Girl, Chromeo, Feist, and other indie and electro acts on one bill.

This is the guide's highest-commitment festival pick: it is a ticketed single-day event with several admission tiers, so it works best when the lineup itself is the reason for the trip. Buy only through the organizer path linked by Visit Pasadena, review the event's entry and bag rules, and leave time for Rose Bowl arrival traffic.

Check the current lineup and ticket path with Visit Pasadena, then use 563 Pasadena listings or the Pasadena dining guide for the rest of the day.

12. Nisei Week in Little Tokyo: best multigenerational cultural weekend

When: the August 22-23 closing weekend. Best for: families, traditional-arts visitors, and anyone who wants a Los Angeles neighborhood festival with several free ways to participate.

The August 22 farmers' market runs 9 a.m.-2 p.m. and the Plaza Festival continues from 11 a.m.-8 p.m.; the Ondo closing celebration fills First Street on August 23 from 4-8 p.m.

Confirm dates with the Nisei Week Foundation, use the complete Little Tokyo planning guide, and browse 61 Los Angeles listings for food and services nearby.

13. Laguna Beach summer arts season: best choose-your-own culture day

When: the strongest current overlap runs August 18-September 3, with Sawdust and Art-A-Fair continuing through September 6. Best for: art lovers who want to combine a museum, festival grounds, promenade music, or one major evening performance.

Pageant of the Masters is the singular ticketed night, while Sawdust emphasizes Laguna Beach makers and Festival of Arts concentrates more than 100 Orange County artists. The Passport to the Arts covers Festival of Arts, Sawdust, and Art-A-Fair but not the Pageant. Laguna Art Museum and Music at the Promenade add lower-commitment ways into the calendar.

Use the complete seven-plan Laguna Beach arts guide for best-for rankings, ticket distinctions, trolley and parking notes, official sources, and links into 24 Laguna Beach listings.

14. Huntington Beach late-August surf, swim, and nature: best active coastal mix

When: recurring programs continue through August 28, Life Rolls On is August 22, and the Huntington Beach Pier Swim is August 29. Best for: visitors who want an active coastal plan with inclusive sports, surf history, nature programs, or a Tuesday market rather than another passive beach day.

The standout is Life Rolls On's adaptive-surf day north of the pier. Athlete and volunteer registration is closed, but spectators are welcome. The Pier Swim adds a half-mile open-water race on August 29. Bolsa Chica contributes a registered Grunion Run on August 29, rotating Junior Rangers topics, and weekly Litter Getters, while the WSL50 exhibition and Surf City Nights create lower-commitment alternatives.

Use the complete six-plan Huntington Beach guide for role, registration, ocean-safety, late-night, parking, and best-for notes. Then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings for food, hotels, shopping, or services nearby.

How to choose quickly

  • Art and coastal culture: choose Laguna Beach; reserve the Pageant first if it anchors the night.
  • Adaptive surf, open-water swim, or coastal nature: choose Huntington Beach and verify whether you are attending as a spectator or registered participant.
  • Compact local day: choose South Pasadena's market, library activities, or Mission Street performance nights.
  • Food crawl: choose Stroll & Savor; add DJs at Taste of Downtown.
  • Destination music: choose Pasadena POPS or Jazz on the Beach.
  • Culture and family activities: choose Nisei Week on August 22-23.
  • Indie festival: choose Just Like Heaven.
  • Free or lower-key outdoors: choose Santa Monica, Glendale, Beverly Hills, or Arcadia.
  • Date night: start with The Gamble House or Concerts on Cañon, then reserve dinner nearby.
