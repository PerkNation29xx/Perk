August 2026 event guide

Eighteen Southern California summer plans worth putting on the calendar.

August stretches from county-fair nights and Laguna Beach art festivals to adaptive surfing, open-water swimming, waterfront food, night markets, garden concerts, outdoor movies, local markets, and Japanese American cultural programming. Orange County now has a complete nine-plan county guide; Huntington Beach, Laguna Beach, Santa Monica, Arcadia, Glendale, Pasadena, Long Beach, South Pasadena, Anaheim, and Little Tokyo also have dedicated planning guides. Every pick is ranked for timing, range, and how easily it can become a complete local day out.

1. OC Fair in Costa Mesa: best all-around summer outing

When: July 17-August 16, Wednesday through Sunday. Best for: mixed-age groups that want rides, food, exhibits, animals, and live entertainment in one place.

This ranks first because it offers the broadest choose-your-own-day format. Families can lean into exhibits and rides; groups of friends can build around concerts and fair food. Visit Anaheim notes that the fair is closed Monday and Tuesday, so check the day before driving and reserve admission if the official site requires it.

Use the complete nine-plan Orange County guide to compare the fair with D23, Laguna Beach arts, free parks programming, coastal events, community festivals, TheFitExpo, and Labor Day weekend planning. Then confirm admission through the official OC Fair visitor page and pair the trip with the Costa Mesa directory, Anaheim directory, or Orange directory.

2. South Pasadena concert and market nights: best compact local loop

When: the certified farmers market runs August 20 and 27; the final Concert in the Park is August 16. Best for: a free picnic concert, a market-and-dinner evening, or a short local plan with minimal driving between stops.

The August 16 New Romantics concert is the one remaining citywide summer event, while the Thursday market, weekly songwriter and musical-theater open mics, a Rose Parade fundraiser, and library programs offer several smaller ways into the city. South Pasadena's 97 live directory listings give the page enough local context to keep the plan useful beyond one event.

Use the complete seven-plan South Pasadena guide, confirm the concert on the official city schedule, and browse 97 South Pasadena listings.

3. New Blues Festival in Long Beach: best month-ending waterfront music

When: August 29-30 at Shoreline Aquatic Park. Best for: blues listeners who want a two-day festival with food trucks, a vendor village, and Queen Mary views.

Visit Long Beach identifies 2026 as the New Blues Festival's tenth anniversary and describes a mix of local and international performers. Compare the daily lineup before choosing a single-day or weekend ticket, then confirm entry time, seating, shade, bag, re-entry, and accessibility rules with the organizer.

Use the complete seven-plan Long Beach August guide to compare the festival with Stroll & Savor, Taste of Downtown, Jazz on the Bay, movies, and family programs. Then review Visit Long Beach's current music overview and browse 208 Long Beach directory listings.

4. Santa Monica swims, movies, and Pier mornings: best coastal reset

When: Sunset Swim returns August 21; Wellness & Waves and Cinema by the Sea run on Saturdays through late August. Best for: a coastal day that can lean toward movement, swimming, local history, a market, or an outdoor movie.

The recurring programs make the rest of the month flexible. Wellness & Waves is listed as free and all ages from 8-10 a.m. at the Pier, while the remaining adult Sunset Swim and the hotel-led movie series require separate planning.

Use the complete six-plan Santa Monica late-summer guide for transit, weather, film, and reservation notes. Then check Wellness & Waves and the official Beach House schedule.

5. Glendale trails, campfire, market, and outdoor film: best low-key local calendar

When: trail and park programs run August 15-30, and Classic Film Under the Stars returns August 29. Best for: residents who want a community-scale outdoor plan without a full festival day.

The remaining calendar ranges from the wilderness shuttle and trail stewardship to a family campfire, the Sunday market, and the Brand Park lawn movie. Choose one foothill or lawn plan and recheck heat, air quality, and the live city listing before leaving.

Use the complete five-plan Glendale August guide, open Glendale's August calendar, and explore 100 Glendale listings.

6. Friday Nights at The Gamble House: best architecture date night

When: second and fourth Fridays through August 28. Best for: architecture fans, quieter date nights, and Pasadena visitors who want a focused cultural stop before dinner.

The appeal is quality over scale: an evening at a landmark property can anchor a Pasadena plan without taking the entire day. Space and access may be limited, so confirm the available date before making dining reservations.

Check Visit Pasadena's event page, then choose among 563 Pasadena directory listings or open the Pasadena dining guide.

7. Concerts on Cañon in Beverly Hills: best free early-evening music

When: Thursdays through August 20, 6-8 p.m. Best for: a no-ticket concert paired with dinner, dessert, or a Golden Triangle walk.

The weekly format makes this one of the easiest backup plans in the guide. The city describes a rotating mix of classic rock, jazz, Caribbean, Brazilian, Persian, and other music. Arrive early if seating matters, and check the specific performer before choosing the night.

Confirm Concerts on Cañon with the city, then browse Beverly Hills or compare the Beverly Hills dining guide.

8. 626 Night Market in Arcadia: best food-first group evening

When: August 14-16 from 3-11 p.m. at Santa Anita Park. Best for: groups that want street food, small vendors, and live entertainment without committing to one seated meal.

The official organizer lists online-only admission, free general parking, and no re-entry. Arrive before the deepest evening crowd, choose a short vendor priority list, and keep water, heat, and the walk back to the car or transit in the plan.

Use the complete Arcadia August guide to compare the night market with the August 21 Arboretum Summer Night and two Pasadena POPS concerts. Then confirm the official 626 Night Market details and browse 40 Arcadia listings.

9. Pasadena POPS at the Arboretum: best picnic concert night

When: August 15 and August 29; picnicking begins at 5:30 p.m. and concerts begin at 7 p.m. Best for: music fans who want to make dinner part of the show rather than rush through it beforehand.

The August programs are an Elton John tribute and an Andrew Lloyd Webber evening. The LA County Arboretum setting is the real planning advantage: arrive when gates open, bring or arrange a picnic within the venue rules, and treat the concert as the center of a slower night. Tickets are required, so check availability before making food plans.

Review the 2026 Pasadena POPS schedule, then compare 563 Pasadena listings, 40 Arcadia listings, or the Pasadena dining guide.

10. Stroll & Savor in Belmont Shore: best casual tasting crawl

When: August 19-20. Best for: groups that would rather sample several 2nd Street businesses than commit to one long dinner.

Visit Long Beach says dozens of eateries set up sidewalk tasting stations, with 12-ticket sheets sold online or at the event. That makes it approachable, but not unlimited: scan the participating stops first, choose a few must-tries, and leave time to walk Belmont Shore between bites.

Plan with Visit Long Beach's summer series, then browse 208 Long Beach directory listings for a full meal, shopping, or services nearby.

11. LA Jazz Festival's Jazz on the Beach: best destination music weekend

When: August 22-23, 10 a.m.-9 p.m. Best for: listeners who want a full-day lineup and a beach setting rather than a short evening set.

Discover Los Angeles lists John Legend, Janelle Monáe, Raphael Saadiq, P-Funk, and more at Dockweiler Beach. This is the higher-commitment music pick: admission is ticketed, the listed price range varies widely, and a long beach day needs advance transit, sun, seating, and return-trip planning.

Check the official tourism listing and organizer link, then explore 61 Los Angeles listings or the Playa del Rey dining guide.

12. Taste of Downtown Long Beach: best food-and-DJ street festival

When: August 22, 2-10 p.m. Best for: people who want local restaurant samples first and KCRW DJ sets later.

The Pine Avenue event combines bite-size food samples, beer, wine, cocktails, and evening music. It ranks below Stroll & Savor for flexibility because it is a single Saturday, but it is the stronger choice if live DJs and a downtown street-festival feel matter as much as the food.

Confirm Taste of Downtown with Visit Long Beach, then use the Long Beach directory or Long Beach dining guide to plan what comes before or after.

13. West Hollywood Summer Sounds: best free soul-and-R&B picnic

When: August 16 at 4 p.m. Best for: listeners who want a free afternoon concert with room for a picnic instead of a ticketed all-day festival.

The Soul Space with Tamara Jade and Orlando Dixon, Mark Evich, and DJ RoseGawd closes the city's 2026 series at Plummer Park. The city says admission is free, RSVPs are encouraged but not required, and seating is not reserved. Bring a park blanket or low-back chair, arrive before the music, and treat food for sale as a convenience rather than a guaranteed full meal.

Confirm Summer Sounds with the City of West Hollywood, then compare the West Hollywood dining guide or browse nearby Los Angeles restaurants.

14. Just Like Heaven in Pasadena: best one-day indie festival

When: August 22 at Brookside at the Rose Bowl. Best for: fans who want The Strokes, LCD Soundsystem, TV Girl, Chromeo, Feist, and other indie and electro acts on one bill.

This is the guide's highest-commitment festival pick: it is a ticketed single-day event with several admission tiers, so it works best when the lineup itself is the reason for the trip. Buy only through the organizer path linked by Visit Pasadena, review the event's entry and bag rules, and leave time for Rose Bowl arrival traffic.

Check the current lineup and ticket path with Visit Pasadena, then use 563 Pasadena listings or the Pasadena dining guide for the rest of the day.

15. Noah Kahan at the Rose Bowl: best stadium singalong

When: August 15; parking and shuttles begin at 2:30 p.m., gates open at 5 p.m., and the show begins at 6:30 p.m. Best for: fans who prefer one major headliner and a clear stadium schedule.

Visit Pasadena lists Gigi Perez as support and publishes an unusually useful event timeline. That makes this easier to plan than a multi-stage festival, but it is still a Rose Bowl crowd: decide on parking or shuttle plans before dinner, keep the afternoon open, and confirm the venue's current ticket and entry rules.

Review the Rose Bowl timeline with Visit Pasadena, then choose from Pasadena directory listings or the Pasadena dining guide.

16. Nisei Week in Little Tokyo: best multigenerational cultural weekend

When: August 15-23, with the strongest published public programs on August 15-16 and 22-23. Best for: families, traditional-arts visitors, and anyone who wants a Los Angeles neighborhood festival with several free ways to participate.

The official schedule now supports a full ranked guide of its own. JANM's free Natsumatsuri Family Festival on August 15 includes bon odori, taiko, improv, music, crafts, and a stamp rally. The Grand Parade runs August 16 from 4-6 p.m.; the August 22 Plaza Festival continues from 11 a.m.-8 p.m.; and the Ondo closing celebration fills First Street on August 23 from 4-8 p.m.

Confirm dates with the Nisei Week Foundation, use the complete Little Tokyo planning guide, and browse 61 Los Angeles listings for food and services nearby.

17. Laguna Beach summer arts season: best choose-your-own culture day

When: the strongest current overlap runs August 10-September 3, with Sawdust and Art-A-Fair continuing through September 6. Best for: art lovers who want to combine a museum, festival grounds, free music, or one major evening performance.

Pageant of the Masters is the singular ticketed night, while Sawdust emphasizes Laguna Beach makers and Festival of Arts concentrates more than 100 Orange County artists. The Passport to the Arts covers Festival of Arts, Sawdust, and Art-A-Fair but not the Pageant. Laguna Art Museum, the final Music in the Park concert, and Music at the Promenade add lower-commitment ways into the calendar.

Use the complete eight-plan Laguna Beach arts guide for best-for rankings, ticket distinctions, trolley and parking notes, official sources, and links into 24 Laguna Beach listings.

18. Huntington Beach late-August surf, swim, and nature: best active coastal mix

When: recurring programs continue through August 28, Life Rolls On is August 22, and the Huntington Beach Pier Swim is August 29. Best for: visitors who want an active coastal plan with inclusive sports, surf history, nature programs, or a Tuesday market rather than another passive beach day.

The standout is Life Rolls On's adaptive-surf day north of the pier, with athlete and volunteer registration open and spectators welcome. The Pier Swim adds a half-mile open-water race on August 29. Bolsa Chica contributes registered Grunion Runs on August 14 and 29, rotating Junior Rangers topics, and weekly Litter Getters, while the WSL50 exhibition and Surf City Nights create lower-commitment alternatives.

Use the complete six-plan Huntington Beach guide for role, registration, ocean-safety, late-night, parking, and best-for notes. Then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings for food, hotels, shopping, or services nearby.

How to choose quickly

  • One big day: choose the OC Fair.
  • Art and coastal culture: choose Laguna Beach; reserve the Pageant first if it anchors the night.
  • Adaptive surf, open-water swim, or coastal nature: choose Huntington Beach and confirm the exact registration role first.
  • Compact local day: choose South Pasadena's market, final park concert, or Mission Street performance nights.
  • Food crawl: choose Stroll & Savor; add DJs at Taste of Downtown.
  • Destination music: choose Pasadena POPS or Jazz on the Beach.
  • Free soul and R&B: choose West Hollywood Summer Sounds.
  • Culture and family activities: choose Nisei Week, especially August 15 or 22.
  • Indie festival: choose Just Like Heaven; for one stadium headliner, choose Noah Kahan.
  • Free or lower-key outdoors: choose Santa Monica, Glendale, Beverly Hills, or Arcadia.
  • Date night: start with The Gamble House or Concerts on Cañon, then reserve dinner nearby.
