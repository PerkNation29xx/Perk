August 2026 event guide

Fourteen Southern California summer plans worth putting on the calendar.

Reviewed by Perk Nation Editorial · Updated August 23, 2026 · How we rank and verify recommendations

Late August stretches from Laguna Beach art festivals and open-water swimming to waterfront concerts, museum exhibitions, outdoor movies, local markets, and Japanese American cultural programming. Orange County now has a complete seven-plan county guide; Huntington Beach, Laguna Beach, Santa Monica, Arcadia, Glendale, Pasadena, Long Beach, South Pasadena, and Little Tokyo also have dedicated planning guides. Every pick is ranked for timing, range, and how easily it can become a complete local day out.

1. South Pasadena crawl, market, and performance nights: best compact local loop

When: the Eat! Shop! Enjoy! crawl's final kickoff day is August 23, the certified farmers market and singer-songwriter open mic return August 27, and Broadway on Mission follows August 28. Best for: a food-and-retail discovery day or a short local plan with minimal driving between stops.

The citywide crawl connects restaurants, retailers, and live music, while the Thursday market and weekly open mics offer smaller ways into the city. South Pasadena's 97 live directory listings give the plan enough local context to stay useful beyond one event.

Use the complete four-plan South Pasadena guide, confirm dates on the South Pasadena Chamber calendar, and browse 97 South Pasadena listings.

2. Long Beach's food, film, and music run: best late-August variety

When: the Naples concert is August 25, Conscience opens August 26, Nas and The Roots play August 28, and the New Blues and Long Beach Film festivals run August 29-30. Best for: people choosing among neighborhood music, theater, film, or destination waterfront concerts.

The useful choice is commitment level: a free neighborhood concert, one theater performance, a film-festival screening cluster, a major amphitheater night, or a full blues-festival day. Confirm reservations, parking, entry, bag, re-entry, and accessibility rules on the current organizer page.

Use the complete five-plan Long Beach August guide to compare all five formats. Then review Visit Long Beach's current August picks and browse 208 Long Beach directory listings.

3. Santa Monica wellness, movies, and Pier mornings: best coastal reset

When: Wellness on the Westside runs August 23; Pier history follows August 27, while Wellness & Waves, markets, and Grease continue through August 29. Best for: a coastal day that can lean toward movement, local history, a market, or an outdoor movie.

Today's five-hour wellness festival is the most concentrated choice. The recurring Pier workout and hotel-led Cinema by the Sea require separate planning, so choose one main commitment instead of crossing the city repeatedly.

Use the complete six-plan Santa Monica late-summer guide for transit, weather, film, and reservation notes. Then check Wellness on the Westside.

4. Glendale concerts, trails, market, and outdoor film: best low-key local calendar

When: Montrose Harvest Market runs August 23, the Brand Park concert returns August 28, Classic Film Under the Stars is August 29, and the wilderness shuttle follows August 30. Best for: residents who want a community-scale outdoor plan without a full festival day.

The remaining calendar ranges from today's Sunday market to the Carl Verheyen Band concert, Brand Park lawn movie, and free wilderness shuttle. Choose one concert, foothill, market, or lawn plan and recheck heat, air quality, and the live listing before leaving.

Use the complete four-plan Glendale August guide, confirm the official Brand Summer Concert Series, and explore 100 Glendale listings.

5. Friday Nights at The Gamble House: best architecture date night

When: second and fourth Fridays through August 28. Best for: architecture fans, quieter date nights, and Pasadena visitors who want a focused cultural stop before dinner.

The appeal is quality over scale: an evening at a landmark property can anchor a Pasadena plan without taking the entire day. Space and access may be limited, so confirm the available date before making dining reservations.

Check Visit Pasadena's event page, then choose among 563 Pasadena directory listings or open the Pasadena dining guide.

6. La Habra Corn Festival: best long-running fair tradition

When: the final day runs August 23 from noon-8 p.m. Best for: families who want classic fair food, rides, shopping, and live entertainment.

The La Habra Host Lions Club's 77th festival continues at El Centro-Lions Park. The parade has concluded, so make rides, food, and entertainment the priorities and consider the cooler late-afternoon hours.

Confirm final-day hours and ride-ticket details with the organizer, then compare the Orange County August guide.

7. Pasadena POPS at the Arboretum: best picnic concert night

When: August 29; picnicking begins at 5:30 p.m. and the concert begins at 7:30 p.m. Best for: music fans who want to make dinner part of the show rather than rush through it beforehand.

Music of the Night closes the Pasadena POPS season with Andrew Lloyd Webber and friends. The LA County Arboretum setting is the real planning advantage: arrive when gates open, bring or arrange a picnic within the venue rules, and treat the concert as the center of a slower night. Tickets are required, so check availability before making food plans.

Review the 2026 Pasadena POPS schedule, then compare 563 Pasadena listings, 40 Arcadia listings, or the Pasadena dining guide.

8. Sea Country Festival in Laguna Niguel: best free-entry community festival

When: the final day is August 23 from 2-9 p.m. Best for: families and groups that want rides, games, food, and live music with no entry charge.

The City of Laguna Niguel lists more than 15 ride attractions, game and craft booths, food, a community tent, and a main-stage lineup that closes with Upstream, 89 Vision, and Red Hot Tribute. Admission is free, while rides, games, and food cost extra. Decide on a spending limit before entering.

Confirm hours, parking, rides, and the music lineup with the city, then compare the Orange County August guide.

9. “This Land Is …” at The Huntington: best art-and-history museum day

When: current through January 11, 2027. Best for: museum visitors who want art, maps, documents, environmental history, and time in the gardens.

Visit Pasadena describes the exhibition as a multi-perspective look at nature, nation, opportunity, dispossession, mapping, ecology, preservation, and repair. It is included with regular Huntington admission and works as a calmer alternative to the final festival weekend.

Check the current exhibition overview and ticket path, then use 563 Pasadena listings or the Pasadena dining guide.

10. Naples Island Concert in the Park: best free neighborhood music

When: August 25 at Colonnade Park. Best for: families and friends who want a neighborhood-scale concert without a festival ticket.

Visit Long Beach lists the Naples Improvement Association's concert as a compact community evening. Confirm the band, start time, allowed lawn setup, and parking on the live listing, then keep dinner on the east side rather than crossing the city.

Confirm the current Naples concert details, then use the Long Beach directory or Long Beach dining guide.

11. Kidspace Campout: best hands-on family day

When: current through September 13. Best for: families with young children who want imaginative, active play instead of a seated performance.

Kidspace's summer program includes trike road trips, crafts, blue-sand “fishing,” campfire sing-alongs, and water-and-wildlife exploration. Choose two activity zones before arriving, bring clothes that can handle messy play, and confirm current museum admission inventory.

Review the current Kidspace Campout listing, then browse Pasadena listings.

12. Nisei Week in Little Tokyo: best multigenerational cultural weekend

When: the final day is August 23. Best for: families, traditional-arts visitors, and anyone who wants a participatory Los Angeles neighborhood-festival finale.

The Ondo and Closing Ceremony fills First Street from 4-8 p.m., with a street closure beginning earlier in the day. Pair it with one JACCC arts stop or a nearby meal rather than trying to recreate Saturday's concluded market-and-plaza schedule.

Confirm dates with the Nisei Week Foundation, use the complete Little Tokyo planning guide, and browse 61 Los Angeles listings for food and services nearby.

13. Laguna Beach summer arts season: best choose-your-own culture day

When: the strongest current overlap runs August 23-September 3, with Sawdust and Art-A-Fair continuing through September 6. Best for: art lovers who want to combine a museum, festival grounds, promenade music, or one major evening performance.

Pageant of the Masters is the singular ticketed night, while Sawdust emphasizes Laguna Beach makers and Festival of Arts concentrates more than 100 Orange County artists. The Passport to the Arts covers Festival of Arts, Sawdust, and Art-A-Fair but not the Pageant. Laguna Art Museum and Music at the Promenade add lower-commitment ways into the calendar.

Use the complete seven-plan Laguna Beach arts guide for best-for rankings, ticket distinctions, trolley and parking notes, official sources, and links into 24 Laguna Beach listings.

14. Huntington Beach late-August surf, swim, and nature: best active coastal mix

When: recurring programs continue through August 28, while the Huntington Beach Pier Swim and a Bolsa Chica Grunion Run land August 29. Best for: visitors who want open-water sports, surf history, nature programs, or a Tuesday market rather than another passive beach day.

The Pier Swim adds a half-mile open-water race on August 29. Bolsa Chica contributes a registered Grunion Run that night, rotating Junior Rangers topics, and weekly Litter Getters, while the WSL50 exhibition and Surf City Nights create lower-commitment alternatives.

Use the complete five-plan Huntington Beach guide for registration, ocean-safety, late-night, parking, and best-for notes. Then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings for food, hotels, shopping, or services nearby.

How to choose quickly

  • Art and coastal culture: choose Laguna Beach; reserve the Pageant first if it anchors the night.
  • Open-water swim or coastal nature: choose Huntington Beach and verify registration, ocean conditions, and late-night access.
  • Compact local day: choose South Pasadena's crawl, market, or Mission Street performance nights.
  • Long Beach variety: choose neighborhood music, theater, film, hip-hop, or blues and keep the day in one geographic cluster.
  • Destination music: choose Pasadena POPS, Nas and The Roots, or the New Blues Festival.
  • Culture and family activities: choose Nisei Week's August 23 finale, Kidspace Campout, or a museum exhibition.
  • Free or lower-key outdoors: choose Santa Monica, Glendale, a community festival, or an OC Parks night.
  • Date night: start with The Gamble House or Cinema by the Sea, then reserve dinner nearby.
