August 2026 event guide

Fourteen Southern California summer plans worth putting on the calendar.

Reviewed by Perk Nation Editorial · Updated August 26, 2026 · How we rank and verify recommendations

Late August stretches from Laguna Beach art festivals and open-water swimming to Burbank food trucks, a rescheduled amphitheater concert, waterfront music, restaurant-week menus, museum exhibitions, outdoor movies, local markets, fitness programming, and Los Angeles jazz. Orange County now has a complete five-plan county guide; Burbank, Huntington Beach, Laguna Beach, Santa Monica, Arcadia, Glendale, Pasadena, Long Beach, and South Pasadena also have dedicated planning guides. Every pick is ranked for timing, range, and how easily it can become a complete local day out.

1. South Pasadena perks, market, and performance nights: best compact local loop

When: Eat! Shop! Enjoy! VIP perks continue through August 30, the certified farmers market and singer-songwriter open mic return August 27, and Broadway on Mission follows August 28. Best for: a food-and-retail discovery day or a short local plan with minimal driving between stops.

The crawl kickoff has concluded, but the Chamber's nine-day wristband-perk window remains active. The Thursday market and weekly open mics offer smaller ways into the city, while South Pasadena's 97 live directory listings give the plan enough local context to stay useful beyond one promotion.

Use the complete four-plan South Pasadena guide, confirm dates on the South Pasadena Chamber calendar, and browse 97 South Pasadena listings.

2. Long Beach's food, film, and music run: best late-August variety

When: Conscience opens August 26, Nas and The Roots play August 28, and the New Blues and Long Beach Film festivals run August 29-30. Best for: people choosing among theater, film, or destination waterfront concerts.

The useful choice is commitment level: one theater performance, a film-festival screening cluster, a major amphitheater night, or a full blues-festival day. Confirm reservations, parking, entry, bag, re-entry, and accessibility rules on the current organizer page.

Use the complete four-plan Long Beach August guide to compare all four formats. Then review Visit Long Beach's current August picks and browse 208 Long Beach directory listings.

3. Santa Monica wellness, movies, and Pier mornings: best coastal reset

When: Pier history is August 27, while Wellness & Waves, markets, and Grease continue through August 29. Best for: a coastal day that can lean toward movement, local history, a market, or an outdoor movie.

The recurring Pier workout and hotel-led Cinema by the Sea require separate planning, so choose one main commitment instead of crossing the city repeatedly.

Use the complete five-plan Santa Monica late-summer guide for transit, weather, film, and reservation notes. Then check the official Pier history listing.

4. Glendale concerts, trails, market, and outdoor film: best low-key local calendar

When: the Brand Park concert returns August 28, Classic Film Under the Stars is August 29, and the wilderness shuttle and Montrose Harvest Market follow August 30. Best for: residents who want a community-scale outdoor plan without a full festival day.

The remaining calendar ranges from the Carl Verheyen Band concert and Brand Park lawn movie to a free wilderness shuttle and year-round Sunday market. Choose one concert, foothill, market, or lawn plan and recheck heat, air quality, and the live listing before leaving.

Use the complete four-plan Glendale August guide, confirm the official Brand Summer Concert Series, and explore 100 Glendale listings.

5. Burbank's final August weekend: best food-to-concert progression

When: Dine LA menus and Magnolia Park Food Truck Fridays run through August 28, followed by the rescheduled Fab Four concert at Starlight Bowl on August 30. Best for: groups choosing between a flexible neighborhood night and one destination outdoor concert.

The weekend has a useful escalation: compare Burbank prix-fixe menus, browse more than 30 food trucks on Magnolia Boulevard, or commit to the city's 6:30 p.m. amphitheater finale. Burbank's 968 live directory listings support a full local plan without inventing extra event claims.

Use the complete three-plan Burbank guide, confirm the concert on the official City of Burbank notice, and browse 968 Burbank listings.

6. Friday Nights at The Gamble House: best architecture date night

When: second and fourth Fridays through August 28. Best for: architecture fans, quieter date nights, and Pasadena visitors who want a focused cultural stop before dinner.

The appeal is quality over scale: an evening at a landmark property can anchor a Pasadena plan without taking the entire day. Space and access may be limited, so confirm the available date before making dining reservations.

Check Visit Pasadena's event page, then choose among 563 Pasadena directory listings or open the Pasadena dining guide.

7. Dine LA Restaurant Week: best final-week dining discovery

When: current through Friday, August 28. Best for: diners comparing prix-fixe menus across Los Angeles County before the summer program closes.

Los Angeles Tourism lists more than 400 participating restaurants across 84 neighborhoods. The useful comparison is not a generic “best restaurant” claim: start with neighborhood, lunch-versus-dinner availability, menu price, and whether the current menu actually suits the group.

Practical review: read the live menu before reserving, note supplements and exclusions, and compare the prix-fixe total with the restaurant's standard menu. Use the Perk Nation city-by-city Dine LA guide or the official participant list.

8. Pasadena POPS at the Arboretum: best picnic concert night

When: August 29; picnicking begins at 5:30 p.m. and the concert begins at 7:30 p.m. Best for: music fans who want to make dinner part of the show rather than rush through it beforehand.

Music of the Night closes the Pasadena POPS season with Andrew Lloyd Webber and friends. The LA County Arboretum setting is the real planning advantage: arrive when gates open, bring or arrange a picnic within the venue rules, and treat the concert as the center of a slower night. Tickets are required, so check availability before making food plans.

Review the 2026 Pasadena POPS schedule, then compare 563 Pasadena listings, 40 Arcadia listings, or the Pasadena dining guide.

9. TheFitExpo Anaheim: best all-in-one fitness weekend

When: August 29-30 at Anaheim Convention Center. Best for: people choosing among competitions, short workouts, wellness education, gear, and guest appearances.

The organizer lists two full days of floor programming, with competitions, seminars, attendee challenges, interactive workouts, exhibitors, and special guests. Some active classes require a liability waiver, and limited-capacity or premium experiences can have separate rules.

Practical review: choose two priorities before entering and leave recovery time between active sessions. Confirm the current floor schedule, ticket inclusion, and waiver requirements on the official Anaheim event page, then browse 35 Anaheim listings.

10. “This Land Is …” at The Huntington: best art-and-history museum day

When: current through January 11, 2027. Best for: museum visitors who want art, maps, documents, environmental history, and time in the gardens.

Visit Pasadena describes the exhibition as a multi-perspective look at nature, nation, opportunity, dispossession, mapping, ecology, preservation, and repair. It is included with regular Huntington admission and works as a calmer alternative to the final festival weekend.

Check the current exhibition overview and ticket path, then use 563 Pasadena listings or the Pasadena dining guide.

11. Kidspace Campout: best hands-on family day

When: current through September 13. Best for: families with young children who want imaginative, active play instead of a seated performance.

Kidspace's summer program includes trike road trips, crafts, blue-sand “fishing,” campfire sing-alongs, and water-and-wildlife exploration. Choose two activity zones before arriving, bring clothes that can handle messy play, and confirm current museum admission inventory.

Review the current Kidspace Campout listing, then browse Pasadena listings.

12. Leimert Park Jazz Festival: best all-day Los Angeles jazz plan

When: Saturday, August 29 from 11:30 a.m.-8:30 p.m. at Baldwin Hills Crenshaw Plaza. Best for: jazz listeners and families who want one destination day with several generations of artists.

Discover Los Angeles lists the seventh annual festival on the outdoor rooftop parking deck, with a program that includes José James presenting the music of John Coltrane and a West Coast Get Down tribute to Ryan Porter.

Practical review: treat the exposed rooftop setting and nine-hour window as part of the plan. Choose the performances that matter most, bring sun protection and water within venue rules, and confirm ticket tier, entry, and Metro guidance on the official tourism listing. Browse 61 Los Angeles listings for nearby food and services.

13. Laguna Beach summer arts season: best choose-your-own culture day

When: the strongest current overlap runs August 26-September 3, with Sawdust and Art-A-Fair continuing through September 6. Best for: art lovers who want to combine a museum, festival grounds, promenade music, or one major evening performance.

Pageant of the Masters is the singular ticketed night, while Sawdust emphasizes Laguna Beach makers and Festival of Arts concentrates more than 100 Orange County artists. The Passport to the Arts covers Festival of Arts, Sawdust, and Art-A-Fair but not the Pageant. Laguna Art Museum and Music at the Promenade add lower-commitment ways into the calendar.

Use the complete seven-plan Laguna Beach arts guide for best-for rankings, ticket distinctions, trolley and parking notes, official sources, and links into 24 Laguna Beach listings.

14. Huntington Beach late-August surf, swim, and nature: best active coastal mix

When: family nature programs continue through August 28, while the Huntington Beach Pier Swim and a Bolsa Chica Grunion Run land August 29. Best for: visitors who want open-water sports, surf history, or nature programs rather than another passive beach day.

The Pier Swim adds a half-mile open-water race on August 29. Bolsa Chica contributes a registered Grunion Run that night, rotating Junior Rangers topics, and weekly Litter Getters, while the WSL50 exhibition offers a lower-commitment cultural alternative.

Use the complete four-plan Huntington Beach guide for registration, ocean-safety, late-night, parking, and best-for notes. Then browse 43 Huntington Beach listings for food, hotels, shopping, or services nearby.

How to choose quickly

  • Art and coastal culture: choose Laguna Beach; reserve the Pageant first if it anchors the night.
  • Open-water swim or coastal nature: choose Huntington Beach and verify registration, ocean conditions, and late-night access.
  • Compact local day: choose South Pasadena's active VIP perks, market, or Mission Street performance nights.
  • Long Beach variety: choose theater, film, hip-hop, or blues and keep the day in one geographic cluster.
  • Destination music: choose the Fab Four at Starlight Bowl, Pasadena POPS, Nas and The Roots, or the New Blues Festival.
  • Culture and family activities: choose the Leimert Park Jazz Festival, Kidspace Campout, or a museum exhibition.
  • Free or lower-key outdoors: choose Santa Monica, Glendale, or an OC Parks night.
  • Date night: start with The Gamble House or Cinema by the Sea, then reserve dinner nearby.
