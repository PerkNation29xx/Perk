Santa Monica guide · October 3-17

Santa Monica has five current October plans built around free art, history, community, movement, and food.

Reviewed by Perk Nation Editorial · Updated October 1, 2026 · How we rank and verify recommendations

A ten-block Route 66 art walk leads the first October weekend, and the newly announced Continuum celebration brings Black Santa Monica history, music, art, and local makers to Historic Belmar Park on October 11. A library film-and-volunteer fair, Pier workouts, and a neighborhood market add focused alternatives. The rankings favor distinctiveness, practical value, and how easily each plan extends into a useful Santa Monica day.

1. Montana Avenue Art Walk: best Route 66 art-and-shopping day

When: October 3, 11 a.m.-6 p.m. Best for: art walkers, families, Route 66 visitors, wellness audiences, shoppers, and groups that prefer an open-ended neighborhood event.

The Route 66 Centennial edition spans ten blocks from 7th to 17th streets with fine art, live installations, performances, curated artisan vendors, wellness experiences, food, family programming, and the avenue's shops. Visit Santa Monica expects more than 10,000 attendees, so treat this as a street festival rather than a quiet gallery walk. Start at one end, choose a lunch zone in advance, and note that the city's closure window extends beyond event hours.

Review Visit Santa Monica's official program, route, and hours, check the city's street-closure notice, then browse Santa Monica businesses.

2. Continuum at Historic Belmar Park: best free music-and-history gathering

When: October 11, noon-5 p.m. Best for: live-music audiences, Black history and culture learners, families, local-history visitors, small-business shoppers, and groups seeking a free all-ages afternoon.

Continuum centers Belmar, one of California's oldest African American seaside communities, through KCRW DJs, live hip-hop, R&B, jazz, and string performances, a vendor village curated by Black Market Flea, a Sam Sanders-led history conversation, art-making, archival installations, and interpretive panels around the park. It ranks just behind the Art Walk because the music and market connect directly to place, history, and Black entrepreneurship. Admission is free, but food and shopping need a separate budget; arrive early for the historical panels, then use the stage schedule to choose the performances and conversation that matter most.

Read the City of Santa Monica's official hours, program, performers, and historical context, then browse Santa Monica businesses or find a nearby meal.

3. Join & Thrive: best free community-and-volunteer afternoon

When: October 3, noon-4 p.m. Best for: documentary viewers, teens and adults exploring volunteer opportunities, community groups, and visitors who want an indoor alternative to the Art Walk.

Santa Monica Public Library and Bahala pair a free screening and discussion of the documentary Join or Die with a Central Courtyard volunteer fair featuring more than a dozen community partners. This ranks below the Art Walk because its audience is narrower, but it offers clearer take-home value for people ready to join a local organization. Arrive before the screening begins, allow time for the discussion, and save the courtyard fair for the second half rather than treating the event as a drop-in movie.

Read the City of Santa Monica's official schedule, partner list, and admission details, then find a Santa Monica meal.

4. Wellness & Waves: best free start to a coastal Saturday

When: Saturdays through October 17, 8-10 a.m. Best for: beginners, families, visitors without a gym membership, and anyone who wants activity before the Pier becomes busiest.

The Santa Monica Pier Corporation's free, all-ages series rotates accessible workouts in an open-air setting. Its value is the combination of zero admission and an early finish: you can move, cool down, and still reach a market or breakfast before midday. Bring water, sun protection, and a layer for marine air; check the weekly class format so the intensity matches your group.

Confirm the current Wellness & Waves session with Visit Santa Monica, then find a Santa Monica meal.

5. Pico Farmers Market: best repeatable food-first morning

When: Saturdays, 8 a.m.-1 p.m. Best for: produce shoppers, picnic planners, families, and visitors who want a low-pressure neighborhood stop.

The Virginia Avenue Park market combines seasonal produce, flowers, bread, cheese, prepared food, and live music in the Pico neighborhood. It ranks below the one-off cultural programs because it is repeatable, but that also makes it the safest fallback if capacity, weather, or timing disrupts another plan. Go early for shopping range or later for lunch energy, and bring a reusable bag plus a separate budget for prepared food.

Confirm the current Pico Farmers Market listing, then browse more Santa Monica food options.

Build a practical Santa Monica day

On October 3, choose the Art Walk for the broadest outdoor day or Join & Thrive for a focused indoor community program; doing both requires leaving the Art Walk early. Choose Continuum on October 11 when music, Black Santa Monica history, and local makers matter more than a shopping walk. On a Saturday through October 17, start with Wellness & Waves and add the Pico market.
