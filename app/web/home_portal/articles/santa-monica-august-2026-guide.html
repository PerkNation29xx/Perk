Santa Monica guide · October 6-24

Santa Monica has six current October plans built around art, history, community, movement, food, and seasonal storytelling.

Reviewed by Perk Nation Editorial · Updated October 6, 2026 · How we rank and verify recommendations

Continuum brings Black Santa Monica history, music, art, and local makers to Historic Belmar Park on October 11. Ghosts of the Miles adds two evenings of local history and seasonal performance at the historic Miles Memorial Playhouse, while a free fire station open house, a coastal photography exhibition, Pier workouts, and a neighborhood market provide useful alternatives. The rankings favor distinctiveness, practical value, and how easily each plan extends into a complete Santa Monica day.

1. Continuum at Historic Belmar Park: best free music-and-history gathering

When: October 11, noon-5 p.m. Best for: live-music audiences, Black history and culture learners, families, local-history visitors, small-business shoppers, and groups seeking a free all-ages afternoon.

Continuum centers Belmar, one of California's oldest African American seaside communities, through KCRW DJs, live hip-hop, R&B, jazz, and string performances, a vendor village curated by Black Market Flea, a Sam Sanders-led history conversation, art-making, archival installations, and interpretive panels around the park. It ranks first because the music and market connect directly to place, history, and Black entrepreneurship. Admission is free, but food and shopping need a separate budget; arrive early for the historical panels, then use the stage schedule to choose the performances and conversation that matter most.

2. Fire Prevention Week Open House: best free hands-on safety stop

When: October 10, 11 a.m.-1 p.m. Best for: families, children who want to see fire engines, e-bike and device owners, safety-minded visitors, and groups seeking a short educational stop before lunch.

Santa Monica Fire Department opens Fire Station 1 for station tours, firefighter conversations, close-up fire-engine access, family activities, and practical lithium-ion battery safety. It ranks ahead of the recurring plans because the two-hour program turns everyday device safety into a hands-on family visit. The city lists no ticket requirement or admission charge; arrive near 11 a.m. for the broadest activity access, keep apparatus paths clear, and treat the visit as an active station open house rather than a museum tour.

Confirm the City of Santa Monica's official hours, address, activities, and battery-safety guidance, then browse Santa Monica businesses or find a nearby meal.

3. Ghosts of the Miles: best local-history Halloween performance

When: October 23-24, starting at 5:30 p.m.; timed sessions through 10:45 p.m. Best for: local-history audiences, theatrical-walk fans, Halloween date nights, architecture lovers, and visitors who want seasonal atmosphere without a large haunted park.

Panda Roja Design brings a weekend of history and seasonal storytelling to the historic Miles Memorial Playhouse with support from Santa Monica's Art of Recovery initiative. It ranks below the two free one-day community programs because it requires a timed ticket, but its smaller venue and local-history focus make it more intimate than a street festival. Choose a session before planning dinner, check the presenter for the recommended age and access route, and allow extra time around Lincoln Boulevard traffic and evening parking.

Review Visit Santa Monica's official dates, hours, venue, and ticket link, then browse Santa Monica businesses or find a nearby meal.

4. Portraits: Faces and Waves: best free flexible photography stop

When: October 3-March 27, 2027; daily except special events. Best for: photography audiences, Pier visitors, coastal-history readers, portraiture students, and groups needing a quieter alternative to a timed festival.

The Annenberg Community Beach House Gallery pairs Sam Comen's portraits of 274 Santa Monica Pier visitors with Anthony Friedkin's photographs of waves made near the Beach House and State Beach. One body of work records who came to the Pier during America's 250th year, while the other treats individual waves as short-lived forms. It ranks below the one-day programs because it can be visited later, but that flexibility is its advantage. Admission is free; check same-day gallery access around special events, allow 30-60 minutes, and combine the show with the beach or a nearby meal.

Read the City of Santa Monica's official dates, artist context, location, and access note, then browse Santa Monica businesses or find a nearby meal.

5. Wellness & Waves: best free start to a coastal Saturday

When: Saturdays through October 17, 8-10 a.m. Best for: beginners, families, visitors without a gym membership, and anyone who wants activity before the Pier becomes busiest.

The Santa Monica Pier Corporation's free, all-ages series rotates accessible workouts in an open-air setting. Its value is the combination of zero admission and an early finish: you can move, cool down, and still reach a market or breakfast before midday. Bring water, sun protection, and a layer for marine air; check the weekly class format so the intensity matches your group.

Confirm the October 10 Wellness & Waves session with Visit Santa Monica, then find a Santa Monica meal.

6. Pico Farmers Market: best repeatable food-first morning

When: Saturdays, 8 a.m.-1 p.m. Best for: produce shoppers, picnic planners, families, and visitors who want a low-pressure neighborhood stop.

The Virginia Avenue Park market combines seasonal produce, flowers, bread, cheese, prepared food, and live music in the Pico neighborhood. It ranks below the one-off cultural programs because it is repeatable, but that also makes it the safest fallback if capacity, weather, or timing disrupts another plan. Go early for shopping range or later for lunch energy, and bring a reusable bag plus a separate budget for prepared food.

Confirm the October 10 Pico Farmers Market listing, then browse more Santa Monica food options.
