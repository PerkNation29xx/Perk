Santa Monica guide · September 18-27

Santa Monica has eight current September plans built around free music, art, culture, movement, history, and food.

1. Thai Fest by the Beach: best free food-and-culture weekend

When: September 19-20. Best for: families, Thai-food fans, multigenerational groups, and visitors who want a free festival beside the Pier.

Thai street food and modern fusion vendors share the waterfront program with traditional music, dance, Muay Thai demonstrations, Asian American performers, family activities, and photo stops. Free admission makes this the guide's strongest one-off value, though food is sold separately and Pier crowds can build. Arrive earlier for shorter vendor lines, choose a few dishes to share, and favor the Metro E Line, rideshare, or a preselected parking structure.

Review the official Thai Fest program and free-admission notice, compare the event in the regional September rankings, then find more Santa Monica food options.

2. Voices of Strength: best free classical-music night

When: September 19, 7-9 p.m. Best for: classical-music listeners, students, date nights, and visitors who want an indoor evening after a daytime festival.

Santa Monica Symphony opens its season at JAMS Performing Arts Center with Shostakovich's Cello Concerto No. 1 and Dvořák's Symphony No. 7. The focused two-hour program makes this easier to plan than an all-day festival, and free admission gives it stronger value than most ticketed concert options. Choose it as a stand-alone evening rather than trying to rush from Thai Fest, arrive early enough to settle before the performance, and confirm any organizer update before leaving.

Check the Visit Santa Monica concert listing and venue details, then browse Santa Monica restaurants.

3. Between Darkness and Dawn: best flexible free art stop

When: Thursday-Sunday, noon-5 p.m., through September 27. Best for: contemporary-art viewers, Airport Arts Center visitors, families seeking a short indoor stop, and groups that need a weather-flexible backup.

The Westside Artists Critique Group exhibition at Propeller Gallery is all ages and free, with recurring afternoon hours that make it the easiest plan in the guide to move around. It ranks below the one-night music events because it is smaller and self-paced, but that is also its advantage: allow 45-90 minutes, combine it with one nearby meal or market, and keep it as a backup if crowds or weather change another outdoor plan.

Confirm a current date on Visit Santa Monica's exhibition schedule, then browse Santa Monica businesses.

4. Wellness & Waves: best free start to a coastal Saturday

When: Saturdays through October 17, 8-10 a.m. Best for: beginners, families, visitors without a gym membership, and anyone who wants activity before the Pier becomes busiest.

The Santa Monica Pier Corporation's free, all-ages series rotates accessible workouts in an open-air setting. Its value is the combination of zero admission and an early finish: you can move, cool down, and still reach a market or breakfast before midday. Bring water, sun protection, and a layer for marine air; check the weekly class format so the intensity matches your group.

Confirm the current Wellness & Waves session with Visit Santa Monica, then find a Santa Monica meal.

5. Hispanic Heritage library doubleheader: best free family learning block

When: September 19, 11 a.m.-2 p.m. across two programs. Best for: families, Spanish learners, dance beginners, and visitors who prefer participatory culture to a stage-only show.

Dance with Baila Baila! runs from 11 a.m.-noon at the Main Library with interactive Spanish learning through music. At noon, the Pico Branch begins a two-hour Mexican ballet folklórico workshop and folk-instrument presentation. Choose the first for younger children, the second for deeper dance and music participation, or both if your group is comfortable moving between locations; check addresses and travel time before trying the doubleheader.

Use the City of Santa Monica's official Hispanic Heritage Month schedule, then browse Santa Monica businesses.

6. dublab Open Air: best free outdoor dance night

When: September 25, 5-9 p.m. Best for: DJ audiences, friend groups, dancers, and visitors looking for a no-ticket Friday evening.

Spiñorita, GANAS, and ANTHNY GBRIEL lead four hours of outdoor music at Christine Emerson Reed Park as part of Santa Monica's Hispanic Heritage Month program. This is the guide's strongest night plan because it is longer and more music-centered than the library sessions. Arrive near the opening if you want an easier meeting point and park setup; bring a layer for the evening and verify any weather update before traveling.

Check the city's official dublab listing, then use Santa Monica restaurant search for dinner before or after.

7. Doors Open California: best free architecture-and-history visit

When: September 26-27, noon-4 p.m. Best for: architecture fans, Old Hollywood history readers, families with older children, and visitors who want a focused afternoon stop.

Free docent-led tours open the Marion Davies Guest House and introduce Julia Morgan's design, the former beachfront estate, and artifacts that are not usually presented together. No reservation is required, which makes this easier to combine with the beach than a timed museum ticket. The city also suggests the Santa Monica Conservancy's Shotgun House as a second history stop; treat that as an optional two-mile add-on rather than assuming the sites are adjacent.

Review the official Doors Open California event page, then compare nearby Santa Monica options.

8. Pico Farmers Market: best repeatable food-first morning

When: Saturdays, 8 a.m.-1 p.m. Best for: produce shoppers, picnic planners, families, and visitors who want a low-pressure neighborhood stop.

The Virginia Avenue Park market combines seasonal produce, flowers, bread, cheese, prepared food, and live music in the Pico neighborhood. It ranks below the one-off cultural programs because it is repeatable, but that also makes it the safest fallback if capacity, weather, or timing disrupts another plan. Go early for shopping range or later for lunch energy, and bring a reusable bag plus a separate budget for prepared food.

Confirm the current Pico Farmers Market listing, then browse more Santa Monica food options.

Important Ocean Way update

The Ocean Way Festival scheduled for September 26-27 has been canceled after storm surf and runoff damaged the beach site. Do not travel to Santa Monica for the festival. The city says the inaugural event is now planned for 2027; ticket holders should follow the organizer's refund instructions.

Read the City of Santa Monica's cancellation notice.

Build a practical Santa Monica day

For September 19, start with Wellness & Waves, choose the Pico market or one library program, then use Thai Fest as the flexible daytime anchor and Voices of Strength as a separate evening choice if the pace works. For September 26-27, use the Guest House tour as the anchor and add the gallery, Pier, or Shotgun House only if travel time suits the group. Before you go

  • Confirm times and weather on the linked official page.
  • Bring water, sun protection, and a coastal layer.
  • Check Metro E Line service or parking before evening programs.
  • Use the directory for a nearby meal or practical stop.
